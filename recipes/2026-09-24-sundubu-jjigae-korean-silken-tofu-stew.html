Sundubu Jjigae: Korean Silken Tofu Stew

Why This Recipe

Korea's ultimate comfort food and a perfect next step in your Korean phase — silky silken tofu broken up in a gochugaru-spiced anchovy broth, finished with a raw egg dropped straight in. Ready in 30 minutes.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup dried anchovies
  • 3 cups water (for the stock)
  • 2 cloves garlic, finely minced
  • 1/2 cup onion, chopped
  • 1 cup kimchi
  • 1 tbsp gochugaru (Korean red pepper powder)
  • 1/2 tbsp sesame oil
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 300g extra-soft (silken) tofu, 1 pack
  • 1 bunch enoki mushrooms
  • 1 egg
  • 1 tbsp canola oil (or neutral oil)
  • green onions, chopped, for topping
  • cooked white rice, to serve

Instructions

Prep

  • To make the anchovy stock, remove the guts from the dried anchovies and soak them in the 3 cups of water for 20 minutes.
  • While the anchovies soak, finely mince the garlic, chop the onion, and slice the green onions.
  • Whisk the gochugaru and sesame oil together in a small bowl and set aside — this keeps the chilli from scorching when it hits the pot.

Cook

  • Bring the anchovy-water to the boil over medium heat and let it bubble for 10 minutes. Strain out the anchovies and keep the broth.
  • In a clay pot or deep soup pot, heat the canola oil over medium heat. Add the onion and garlic and cook until soft and fragrant — keep it on medium so they don't brown.
  • Add the kimchi and cook until it softens, about 3–5 minutes.
  • Pour in the anchovy broth and soy sauce and bring to a boil. Once boiling, stir in the gochugaru–sesame oil mixture and return to a boil.
  • Slide in the silken tofu and gently break it into rough pieces with a spoon. When the stew boils up again, add the enoki mushrooms and cook for about 30 seconds.

Finish & Serve

  • Crack the egg straight into the boiling stew and remove from the heat while it's still bubbling — the residual heat sets the egg to a soft, runny state.
  • Top with chopped green onions and serve immediately with a big bowl of white rice to scoop up the broth.

Pro Tips

  • The raw-egg finish is the signature move — don't let it fully set. You want the yolk runny so you can fold it into the broth and rice for that silky, velvety texture.
  • No dried anchovies? A good dashi-style fish stock or even mushroom stock works, but the anchovy broth is where the deep umami comes from — worth the 20-minute soak if you can. For a vegetarian version, swap the anchovies for kombu (kelp) and a splash of soy.
