Celeriac Steaks with Café de Paris Sauce

Why This Recipe

Celeriac is at its autumn peak and this is the perfect hands-off dinner - two and a half hours of slow roasting with almost zero effort, and a split butter-cream Café de Paris sauce that looks genuinely restaurant-level on the plate.

Ingredients

  • 2 large celeriac (900g), hairy roots discarded, scrubbed clean (no need to peel)
  • 120ml olive oil, plus a little extra for brushing
  • 3 tsp flaked sea salt, plus more to season
  • 110g unsalted butter, cut into 2cm cubes
  • 1 small shallot, finely chopped (25g)
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • 3 anchovy fillets, drained and finely chopped (optional)
  • 1/2 tsp medium curry powder
  • 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper
  • 1 tbsp mustard powder
  • 2 tbsp baby capers
  • 2 tbsp finely chopped chives
  • 2 tbsp finely chopped tarragon
  • 1 tbsp finely chopped parsley
  • 2 tsp thyme leaves
  • 110ml single or whipping cream
  • 2 tsp lemon juice

Instructions

Roast the Celeriac

  • Preheat the oven to 170C fan.
  • Pierce the celeriac with a fork all over, about 40 times, and place on a parchment-lined baking tray.
  • Mix the olive oil with the 3 tsp flaked salt, then rub the celeriac generously with the oil mixture.
  • Roast for a minimum of 2 hr 15 min, up to 2 hr 45 min depending on size, basting with the pan juices every 20 minutes, until deeply browned, soft all the way through and oozing a celeriac caramel.
  • Leave to rest for 15 minutes, then cut widthways into 2.5cm-thick steaks. Brush each cut side with the oil and caramel left on the tray, adding a little more oil if needed to coat.

Make the Café de Paris Sauce

  • Put the butter, shallot, garlic, anchovies, curry powder, cayenne and mustard powder with 1/4 tsp flaked salt into a small saucepan over medium heat.
  • Cook for about 6 minutes, swirling, until the shallots have softened and the butter has melted and gone golden and caramelised.
  • Add the capers, chopped herbs and a very generous grind of black pepper, cook for 1 minute, then remove from the heat.

Grill the Steaks

  • Turn the oven to its highest grill setting.
  • Arrange the celeriac steaks spaced apart on a large parchment-lined tray in a single layer (brush with extra oil plus a little honey or maple syrup if the cut sides are dry).
  • Grill on the top shelf until golden-brown on top, 6-8 minutes. Leave the tray in the now-off oven to keep warm while you finish the sauce.

Finish & Serve

  • Return the sauce to medium heat, gently cook for 1 minute, then add the cream and lemon juice. Swirl for another 2 minutes until warm - do not mix too much, you want the sauce split, not emulsified.
  • Pour the sauce onto a large platter with a lip and arrange the celeriac steaks on top (or plate individually with the rest of the sauce served alongside).
  • Finish with a little flaked salt and black pepper and serve immediately.

Pro Tips

  • The whole-roast method is the key: roasting the celeriac whole caramelises the outside and steams the inside, then slicing releases a sweet celeriac glaze to baste the cut faces before grilling.
  • The split sauce is deliberate - if it emulsifies into a smooth cream sauce, it loses the rustic Café de Paris texture. A quick burst under the grill at the end of cooking helps bind it without overworking.
