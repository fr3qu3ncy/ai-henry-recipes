Lomo Saltado (Peruvian Beef Stir-Fry)

Why This Recipe

Peru's undisputed national dish and a perfect bridge for a Korean-stir-fry week: seared sirloin flash-fried with red onion, tomato and ají amarillo in a soy-vinegar sauce, tossed over crispy fries and served with rice. It's chifa at its finest — the Chinese-Peruvian fusion that basically invented the stir-fry-and-fries-on-the-same-plate idea.

Ingredients

  • 500g (18 oz) sirloin steak, cut into strips or cubes
  • 200g white rice, cooked
  • 300g thick-cut fries, ready-fried
  • 1 red onion, chopped into chunky slices
  • 2 tomatoes, cut into wedges
  • 1 ají amarillo chilli, finely sliced (veins and seeds removed)
  • 1 tsp fresh garlic paste
  • 4 tbsp soy sauce
  • 4 tbsp plain white vinegar
  • 120ml (1/2 cup) beef stock
  • 2 tbsp olive oil (plus more for the marinade)
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1/4 tsp ground oregano
  • 1 tsp freshly crushed black pepper
  • Handful of coriander leaves, for garnish
  • Salt to taste

Instructions

Prep

  • Cut the sirloin into 2-3cm strips and marinate in the cumin, 4 tbsp vinegar, 1 tbsp olive oil, black pepper, soy sauce and a pinch of salt for at least 10 minutes.
  • Cook the white rice and set aside.
  • Fry the thick-cut potato slices in oil until crisp and golden, then drain on paper towels and season. Keep warm.

Cook

  • Heat a wok or heavy pan with 1 tbsp olive oil until smoking — the wok needs to be at a very high heat.
  • Remove the beef from the marinade (strain off the excess liquid, you don't want it steaming in the wok) and sear for 4-5 minutes until sealed and charred at the edges. Remove and set aside.
  • In the same wok, add the red onion, sliced ají amarillo and garlic paste and stir-fry for 1 minute, constantly moving everything around.
  • Pour in the beef stock and let it bubble and reduce for about 30 seconds.
  • Add the tomato wedges and stir-fry for a further 30 seconds — they should soften but keep their shape, not turn to mush.

Serve

  • Tip the beef back into the wok with the crispy fries and scatter over the ground oregano.
  • Toss everything for 10 seconds to marry the flavours and coat the fries in the sauce.
  • Serve immediately on top of the white rice, scattered with coriander leaves.

Pro Tips

  • No ají amarillo available? It's a mild, fruity Peruvian yellow chilli — a finely sliced yellow or mild green chilli works, or 1/2 tsp smoked paprika plus a pinch of chilli powder in the marinade gets close.
  • The key technique is high heat and dry beef: pat the strips very dry and don't crowd the wok, or they'll steam instead of sear. The soy-vinegar-juice glaze you're going for only forms when the wok is screaming hot.
