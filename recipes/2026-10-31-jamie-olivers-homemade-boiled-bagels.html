Jamie Oliver's Homemade Boiled Bagels

Why This Recipe

A proper bread-making project for a cold late-October Saturday — the boiling-in-bicarb-water step is the trick that gives you that authentic chewy, shiny bagel texture at home, and they freeze beautifully.

Ingredients

  • 750g strong white bread flour
  • 1 x 7g sachet instant yeast
  • 3 tsp soft brown sugar
  • 3 tsp fine sea salt
  • 450ml tepid water
  • 1 tsp bicarbonate of soda
  • 1 large free-range egg, for glazing
  • 2 tbsp sesame, poppy, pumpkin or sunflower seeds, optional

Instructions

Prep

  • Place flour, yeast, sugar and salt in a large bowl (or mixer bowl with dough hook).
  • Make a well in the centre and pour in the 450ml tepid water; add the yeast to the water.
  • Gradually work the flour in with a fork until you cannot move it anymore, then gather into a ball on a floured surface.
  • Knead for about 10 minutes — pushing and stretching — until the stiff dough is silky, smooth and elastic.
  • Place in a lightly oiled bowl, cover with a damp tea towel, and prove for 1.5-2 hours in a warm draught-free spot until doubled in size.

Shape

  • Preheat the oven to 200C/400F/gas 6 and line 2 large baking trays with baking paper.
  • Punch the air out of the dough and divide into 12 equal balls.
  • Push a hole in the centre of each ball with your thumb, then gently spin each on your two index fingers to stretch into a ring with a hole about 2cm wide.

Boil

  • Bring a large shallow saucepan of water to a boil, reduce to a simmer, and whisk in the bicarbonate of soda and a small pinch of salt.
  • Cook the bagels in batches of six for 1 minute on each side.
  • Lift out with a slotted spoon, drain off the water, and place on the lined trays.

Bake and Serve

  • Beat the egg and brush over the bagels; sprinkle with seeds if using.
  • Bake for 20-25 minutes until deep golden brown.
  • Cool completely on a wire rack. Slice with a serrated knife and fill — peanut butter and jam, smoked salmon and cream cheese, or salt beef, mustard and pickles.

Pro Tips

  • The bicarb-boil is non-negotiable — it gelatinises the surface starch, which is what gives bagels their chewy interior and glossy, crisp crust. Skip it and you get a plain bun.
  • Unbaked shaped bagels freeze brilliantly — pop them on a tray, freeze solid, then bag them up. Boil straight from frozen (add 30 seconds per side) and bake as normal. Weekend bakery, zero morning effort.
