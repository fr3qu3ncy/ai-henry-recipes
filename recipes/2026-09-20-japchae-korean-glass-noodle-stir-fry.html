Japchae (Korean Glass Noodle Stir-Fry)

Why This Recipe

Korea's beloved glass noodle stir-fry — bouncy sweet-potato noodles, beef and a rainbow of veggies tossed in a savory-sweet sesame sauce. A perfect fit for your Korean phase: one pan, minimal washing up, and a big celebration-style dish that's surprisingly easy to nail.

Ingredients

  • 250g Korean sweet potato starch noodles (dangmyeon)
  • 100g rib eye fillet, cut into strips
  • 1 carrot, peeled and julienned
  • 110g baby spinach, rinsed
  • 1/4 red capsicum (bell pepper), julienned
  • 1/2 yellow onion, thinly sliced
  • 100g fresh shiitake mushrooms, stems removed and thinly sliced
  • 1 egg (extra large), optional
  • Cooking oil (rice bran oil)
  • Fine sea salt, to taste
  • Beef marinade: 1 Tbsp soy sauce, 1 tsp rice wine (mirin), 1/2 tsp minced garlic, 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
  • Noodles & mushroom marinade: 4 Tbsp soy sauce, 1 Tbsp honey, 1 Tbsp brown sugar, 1 Tbsp toasted sesame oil, 1/8 tsp ground black pepper
  • Spinach seasoning: 1/4 tsp fine sea salt, 1/2 tsp minced garlic, 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
  • To finish: 1 Tbsp toasted sesame oil, 1 Tbsp toasted sesame seeds

Instructions

Prep

  • Mix the beef marinade (1 Tbsp soy sauce, 1 tsp mirin, 1/2 tsp minced garlic, 1 tsp toasted sesame oil) into the beef strips in a bowl. Cover with cling film and set aside while you prep the rest.
  • Julienned the carrot and red capsicum; thinly slice the onion; thinly slice the shiitake mushrooms (stems removed).
  • Whisk the noodles & mushroom marinade: 4 Tbsp soy sauce, 1 Tbsp honey, 1 Tbsp brown sugar, 1 Tbsp toasted sesame oil and a pinch of black pepper. Mix 1 Tbsp of this into the sliced mushrooms and set aside.
  • Blanch the spinach in rolling boiling water for 5-10 seconds, drain, and shock under cold tap water. Squeeze out excess water, then toss with the spinach seasoning (1/4 tsp salt, 1/2 tsp minced garlic, 1 tsp toasted sesame oil).
  • Boil the glass noodles in a large pot of boiling water for 6-7 minutes. Drain, rinse under cold water and let drain 1-2 minutes. Cut to 15-20cm lengths with kitchen scissors, then toss with the remaining noodle & mushroom marinade and set aside.

Cook

  • In one well-heated non-stick pan, cook in order from lightest to darkest colour (this minimises colour transfer and washing up): first the onion, then carrot, red capsicum and shiitake, seasoning lightly with salt as you go. Transfer each to your large mixing bowl as it is done.
  • Add a little oil to the pan and sear the marinated beef strips until cooked through (1-2 minutes per side). Transfer to the mixing bowl.
  • Optional: beat the egg, then cook the egg white in a little oil over low heat for 1-2 minutes per side; repeat with the yolk. Thinly slice both into matchsticks and add to the bowl.

Serve

  • Add the finished noodles and mushrooms to the mixing bowl with the cooked vegetables, beef and egg.
  • Drizzle over the final 1 Tbsp toasted sesame oil and the toasted sesame seeds, then gently toss everything together with your hands (or a salad server if still hot) until evenly coated.
  • Serve with a bowl of Korean rice and your choice of banchan (side dishes) for a proper Korean-style meal.

Pro Tips

  • Rinse and drain the cooked glass noodles well before marinating — this keeps them chewy and glossy instead of soggy and sticking together.
  • Cook the vegetables lightest-to-darkest in a single pan to keep colours bright and cut down on washing up; a splash of water or a drop of oil between veggies stops anything burning.
