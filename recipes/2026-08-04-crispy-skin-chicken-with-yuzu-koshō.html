Crispy Skin Chicken with Yuzu Koshō

Why This Recipe

Japanese crispy skin chicken using yuzu koshō — a fermented citrus-chilli paste that's a revelation. No-oil technique gives restaurant-quality crispy skin in 20 minutes, a new technique for your repertoire.

Ingredients

  • 2 chicken thigh fillets, skin on (150-175g/5.3-6.2oz each)
  • 2 tsp yuzu koshō
  • 1 large pinch salt
  • 1 pinch black pepper
  • 130g/4.6oz cabbage, cut into 2.5cm x 8cm strips
  • 30g/1.1oz snow peas, ends removed

Instructions

Prep

  • Pat chicken fillets thoroughly dry with kitchen paper — removing as much moisture as possible is key to crispy skin.
  • If the fillet thickness is uneven, cut into the thick part horizontally without cutting all the way through, then open it so the thickness is even throughout.
  • Sprinkle salt and pepper mainly on the skin side of the chicken.
  • Spread yuzu koshō evenly over the flesh side of the chicken.

Cook

  • Place chicken skin-side down in a non-stick frying pan. Cover with a lid.
  • Turn heat to medium and cook for 8 minutes with the lid on — the skin renders fat and becomes crispy without any added oil.
  • Remove the lid, flip the fillets over (the skin should be golden and crispy), and cook for a further 2 minutes.
  • Transfer fillets to a tray or cutting board, skin-side up, and let rest for a couple of minutes.

Vegetables

  • While the chicken rests, add the cabbage strips and snow peas to the same frying pan (don't wipe it clean — you want that flavoured oil).
  • Sauté over medium heat for a couple of minutes until tender-crisp.
  • Transfer vegetables to a serving plate.

Serve

  • Slice the chicken fillets into strips about 1.5cm/⅝" wide.
  • Arrange the chicken strips on top of the vegetables.
  • Serve immediately while the skin is still crispy.

Pro Tips

  • Drying the chicken skin thoroughly before cooking is the single most important step — moisture is the enemy of crispiness. Pat it completely dry, even twice.
  • Yuzu koshō is available at Japanese/Asian grocery stores and online. If you can't find it, a substitute would be a mix of citrus zest (lemon or orange) with a mild chilli paste, though it won't be quite the same.
