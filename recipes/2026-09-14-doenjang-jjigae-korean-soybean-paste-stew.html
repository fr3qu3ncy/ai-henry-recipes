Doenjang Jjigae (Korean Soybean Paste Stew)

Why This Recipe

Doenjang jjigae is the soul food of Korean home cooking — a deeply umami, miso-like fermented broth that's cheap, quick, and endlessly comforting. It's the perfect next step into your Korean phase: one bold pantry staple (doenjang) turns everyday veg, tofu and a little brisket into a complete dinner in under half an hour.

Ingredients

  • 160g beef brisket (or any stew cut), sliced paper-thin
  • 2 tbsp rice wine (mirim)
  • Cracked black pepper
  • 3.5 cups (830ml) rice water (from rinsing rice)
  • 3 tbsp doenjang (Korean fermented soybean paste)
  • 1 tbsp ssamjang (Korean spicy dipping paste)
  • 2 tsp gochugaru (Korean chilli flakes)
  • 1 tsp garlic, minced
  • 100g potato, thinly sliced
  • 90g courgette, thinly sliced
  • 35g onion, thinly sliced
  • 100g enoki mushrooms, stems trimmed and separated
  • 45g shiitake mushrooms, thinly sliced
  • 250g soft/medium tofu, cut into medium rectangles
  • 20g spring onions, thinly sliced
  • 2-3 fresh chillies (optional), green and red, thinly sliced
  • Steamed rice, to serve

Instructions

Prep

  • Slice the beef paper-thin and into bite-size pieces — the thinner it is, the quicker it cooks through.
  • Thinly slice the potato, courgette, onion and shiitake; trim and separate the enoki; cut the tofu into medium rectangles; slice the spring onions and chillies.
  • Combine the beef with the rice wine and a few cracks of black pepper in a bowl and mix well.

Braise

  • Preheat a medium pot over medium-high heat for a minute, then add the beef (no oil needed — it brings its own fat) and stir until it browns, about 2 minutes.
  • Add the sliced potato and stir for about 2 minutes until lightly coated and starting to soften.

Simmer

  • Pour in the rice water and bring to a boil.
  • Dissolve the doenjang and ssamjang in a small sieve over the pot and strain in — straining keeps the broth smooth and lump-free.
  • Stir in the gochugaru and garlic. Once the water is at a rolling boil, add the courgette and onion and boil 1 minute.
  • Add the mushrooms and tofu and boil 1 minute, then the spring onions and chillies for a final 1 minute. Remove from the heat.

Serve

  • Ladle the stew into bowls and serve with steamed rice. Korean banchan (side dishes) — kimchi, seasoned seaweed, pickles — round it out if you've got any in the fridge.

Pro Tips

  • Strain the doenjang through a sieve into the boiling water — it looks like you're adding a sludge, but it dissolves into a silky, deeply savoury broth with no lumps.
  • Rice water isn't a gimmick: the starch rounds out the sharp saltiness of the doenjang and gives the broth a proper body. Don't skip it.
