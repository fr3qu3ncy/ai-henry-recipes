Slow-Roast Pork Belly with Quince

Why This Recipe

Quince is at its peak right now — fragrant, tart, and perfect for caramelising slowly in the oven. The classic technique of resting the pork on a bed of potatoes means zero wasted roast juices, and the deglazed gravy is the star of the show.

Ingredients

  • 3 tbsp olive oil, plus extra for greasing
  • 1 section of bone-in pork belly, about 2.5kg
  • 1 tbsp fine sea salt
  • 2 medium potatoes, sliced 2cm thick
  • 2 sprigs fresh sage or rosemary, roughly chopped
  • 5 garlic cloves, roughly chopped
  • 1 tbsp balsamic vinegar
  • 2 quinces (or tart eating apples, e.g. Granny Smith), peeled, cored and quartered
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • 2 medium onions, quartered
  • 100ml cider or dry white wine
  • 1 tbsp butter
  • Black pepper, to season

Instructions

Prep

  • Preheat the oven to 200C/fan 180C/gas 6 and lightly grease a roasting tray with olive oil.
  • Score the pork belly skin all over with a sharp knife and rub the fine sea salt into the skin only — not the flesh.
  • Slice the potatoes 2cm thick and lay them in the middle of the tray.
  • Scatter the chopped sage/rosemary and garlic over the potatoes, dress with 3 tbsp oil and the balsamic vinegar, then place the pork on top, skin side up.
  • Tuck any protruding potatoes under the belly so it sits on a snug bed of spuds.

Cook

  • Bake uncovered for 30 minutes, then lower the heat to 160C/fan 140C/gas 3 and cook for a further 30 minutes.
  • Meanwhile, peel, core and quarter the quinces and toss them in the lemon juice; add the quartered onions.
  • After 1 hour of roasting, add the quince and onions to the tray around the pork.
  • Return to the oven for 40 minutes to 1 hour, until the meat is tender and juicy with crisp crackling.
  • Rest the pork, loosely covered with foil, for 10 minutes before carving.

Finish

  • Remove the potatoes, quince and onions from the tray and arrange in a side bowl, covered with foil to keep warm.
  • Set the roasting tray and its juices over a gentle heat on the hob; skim off some of the fat.
  • Deglaze the pan with the cider or wine and the butter, scraping up the browned bits.
  • Season with black pepper and strain the gravy into a jug.

Serve

  • Carve off the crackling in one piece and slice it; carve the meat off the bone in one piece and slice.
  • Divide between plates with the roasted potatoes, quince and onions, and a good pour of the cider-gravy.

Pro Tips

  • If quince is scarce, tart Granny Smith apples work as a stand-in — the key is acidity to cut through the pork's fat, not sweetness.
  • Salt the skin the day before if you can — it dries the skin out and guarantees shatteringly crisp crackling.
