Birria Tacos

Why This Recipe

The signature red-tinged, crispy-folded taco that took Mexico and the world by storm — braised beef in a chile-spiked consommé you dip them in. A weekend project with a real technique (adobo paste + birria oil) and a serving that looks like a restaurant, great for a dinner party.

Ingredients

  • 25g (0.9oz) guajillo chillies, dried (~4 pieces, heaped 3/4 cup chopped)
  • 45g (1.6oz) ancho chillies, dried (~2-3 pieces, 1 cup chopped)
  • 6g (0.2oz) chillies de arbol, dried (~6 pieces, 2 tbsp chopped)
  • 1/2 cup chilli soaking water (reserved after simmering)
  • 5 garlic cloves, peeled
  • 1 small onion, roughly sliced (~tennis ball size)
  • 1 medium tomato, roughly sliced (180g)
  • 2 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1/8 tsp black pepper
  • 5 tbsp vegetable or canola oil
  • 1.3kg (2.6lb) chuck beef, cut into 6 large pieces (or 1.8kg bone-in beef short ribs)
  • 2 cups low-sodium beef stock / broth
  • 10 whole cloves (or 1/4 tsp ground cloves)
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 3 bay leaves (fresh preferred, else dried)
  • 2 tbsp apple cider vinegar (or white/wine vinegar)
  • 2 1/2 tsp kosher salt (halve for table salt)
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt (for shredding the beef)
  • 20-25 corn tortillas (14.5cm/6in wide)
  • 1 white onion, diced
  • 1/2 cup finely chopped coriander / cilantro
  • 3 1/2 cups (350g) shredded Colby, Monterey Jack or Oaxaca cheese
  • Lime wedges, optional
  • Salsa or hot sauce, optional

Instructions

Prep the Chilli Adobo

  • Deseed the chillies — trim and discard the stems, cut lengthwise, and scoop out the seeds. Roughly chop into 1-1.5cm / 1/4in pieces (wear gloves if you're sensitive to chilli).
  • Put the chopped chillies in a large saucepan of boiling water and simmer rapidly for 10 minutes until the flesh is mushy but the skin stays intact.
  • Reserve 1/2 cup of the chilli-simmering water, then drain the chillies in a sieve and lightly press out the residual liquid.
  • Blitz the cooked chillies, reserved 1/2 cup chilli water, garlic, sliced onion, tomato, oregano, cumin and black pepper in a tall jug (or food processor/NutriBullet) for 20 seconds until as smooth as possible — this is your birria adobo paste.

Braise the Beef

  • Heat the 5 tbsp oil in a large heavy-based pot (e.g. 24cm/9.6in Dutch oven) over high heat. Add half the beef and sear aggressively until dark golden brown, about 1-1 1/2 minutes per side. Remove and repeat with the rest.
  • Lower the heat and add the adobo paste to the same pan, stirring for 2 minutes to cook off the raw flavour (if it spits, lower the heat).
  • Add the beef stock, whole cloves, cinnamon stick, bay leaves, apple cider vinegar and the 2 1/2 tsp salt, then return the beef. Bring to a boil, then lower to a very gentle simmer.
  • Cover with a lid and cook 2 1/2 hours (3-3.5 hrs for short ribs), until the beef shreds easily.

Shred & Make the Consommé

  • Remove the beef to a clean pan and shred well. Add the remaining 1/2 tsp salt and toss to distribute.
  • Skim off as much of the red oil floating on the braising liquid as possible (ideally 6 tbsp+) into a separate bowl — this is your 'Birria Oil', precious stuff.
  • Pour 1 cup of the braising liquid ('Birria Consommé') into a large non-stick pan over medium-high heat. Simmer 15 seconds, add all the beef and toss until the liquid is absorbed and almost none is pooled. Return the beef to its pan.
  • Taste the remaining Consommé and adjust salt — it should be on the slightly salty side (it's for dipping) but drinkable as a small cup of soup. Keep warm for serving.

Assemble & Serve

  • Heat 1 tsp Birria Oil in the non-stick pan over medium-low heat. Lay in a tortilla and 'wipe' it around to paint the underside red — about 10 seconds until soft, no need to flip. Remove, red side down. Repeat for each tortilla using 1/2 tsp oil each.
  • Spread cheese on half of each tortilla, top with the shredded beef, then the diced onion and coriander.
  • Fold each tortilla over and pan-fry 3 at a time over medium-high heat for 2 minutes per side until golden-red and crispy.
  • Serve hot with a bowl of the warm Birria Consommé for dunking, plus lime wedges and salsa. (Drink the leftover Consommé — it's the best soup broth you'll have this year.)

Pro Tips

  • The two liquids are the whole show: the rich red 'Birria Oil' is for painting the tortillas, and the savvier 'Consommé' is for dunking the crispy tacos. Keep them separate — the Consommé is your dipping soup and it's genuinely worth drinking on its own.
  • Oaxaca or a Monterey Jack/Colby blend gives the best stretch and browning. If you want a true Mexican touch, use a good-quality pepper jack or a grating of aged Oaxaca for a smokier pull. Short ribs (bone-in) are richer and pull apart cleaner than chuck — just allow the extra 30-60 minutes.
