Japchae (Korean Glass Noodle Stir-Fry)

Why This Recipe

You've been deep in Korean territory lately (gyeran-ppang and all), and japchae is the undisputed king of Korean noodle dishes — chewy sweet-potato glass noodles, one-pan stir-fry method, and that finishing toss of toasted sesame oil. It's a proper celebratory dish that doubles as a great party food, and it's still good the next day.

Ingredients

  • 250g Korean sweet potato starch noodles (dangmyeon)
  • 100g ribeye fillet, cut into strips
  • 1 carrot, peeled and julienned
  • 110g baby spinach
  • 1/4 red bell pepper, julienned
  • 1/2 yellow onion, thinly sliced
  • 100g fresh shiitake mushrooms, stems removed and thinly sliced
  • 1 Tbsp soy sauce (beef marinade)
  • 1 tsp rice wine (mirin) (beef marinade)
  • 1/2 tsp minced garlic (beef marinade)
  • 1/4 tsp ground black pepper (beef marinade)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (beef marinade)
  • 4 Tbsp soy sauce (noodle & mushroom marinade)
  • 1 Tbsp honey (noodle & mushroom marinade)
  • 1 Tbsp brown sugar (noodle & mushroom marinade)
  • 1 Tbsp toasted sesame oil (noodle & mushroom marinade)
  • 1/8 tsp ground black pepper (noodle & mushroom marinade)
  • 1/4 tsp fine sea salt (spinach seasoning)
  • 1/2 tsp minced garlic (spinach seasoning)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (spinach seasoning)
  • 1 Tbsp toasted sesame oil (finishing)
  • 1 Tbsp toasted sesame seeds (finishing)
  • 1 extra-large egg, white and yolk separated (optional, for classic presentation)
  • 1-2 Tbsp neutral cooking oil (for stir-frying)

Instructions

Prep

  • Mix the beef marinade: soy sauce, mirin, garlic, black pepper and 1 tsp sesame oil. Toss with the beef strips, cover and set aside while you prep the rest.
  • Mix the noodle & mushroom marinade: 4 Tbsp soy sauce, honey, brown sugar, 1 Tbsp sesame oil and black pepper in a small bowl.
  • Toss the sliced shiitake with 1 Tbsp of the noodle marinade and set aside.
  • Julienne the carrot and red pepper, slice the onion. Blanch the spinach in rolling boiling water for 5-10 seconds, drain, shock under cold water and squeeze out the excess moisture.
  • Season the spinach with salt, garlic and 1 tsp sesame oil; fold together and set aside.

Cook

  • Boil the dangmyeon noodles in a large pot of boiling water for 6-7 minutes until pliable. Drain, rinse under cold water and let drain 1-2 minutes.
  • Cut the noodles into 15-20 cm (6-8 inch) lengths with kitchen scissors, toss with the remaining noodle marinade and set aside to absorb (about 10 minutes).
  • If doing the classic egg garnish: beat the egg white, fry in a well-oiled low-heat pan for 1-2 minutes per side, transfer to a board. Repeat with the egg yolk.
  • In the same pan over low-medium heat with a little more oil, stir-fry the onion with a pinch of salt until softened (1-2 min). Transfer to a large bowl.
  • Add oil, stir-fry the carrot with a pinch of salt until softened (1-2 min). Transfer.
  • Repeat with the red pepper until softened (1-2 min). Transfer.
  • Add oil, stir-fry the marinated mushrooms (with the sauce from the bowl) until cooked (1-2 min). Transfer.
  • Add oil, stir-fry the marinated beef over medium heat until cooked through (2-3 min). Transfer.
  • Add oil, stir-fry the marinated noodles over low-medium heat until well coated and glossy (2-3 min). Transfer to the bowl with the vegetables.

Finish

  • Slice the fried egg white and yolk into matchsticks and add to the bowl.
  • Pour over the final 1 Tbsp toasted sesame oil and scatter with sesame seeds.
  • Toss everything together with your hands (or a large salad server if it's still hot) until every strand of noodle is coated in the fragrant oil.

Serve

  • Serve with a bowl of rice and your favourite Korean side dishes — or straight from the bowl, it works as a standalone feast.

Pro Tips

  • Pre-cut dangmyeon noodles (30-40 cm) save you most of the scissor work — worth seeking out at the Asian supermarket. If you can only find dried shiitake, soak for a few hours first.
  • The whole trick of this recipe is colour: cook lighter-coloured vegetables before darker ones so nothing turns muddy, and you only ever use one pan. It looks like a party dish but it's really just one skillet.
