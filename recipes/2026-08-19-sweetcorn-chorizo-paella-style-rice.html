Sweetcorn & Chorizo Paella-style Rice

Why This Recipe

Peak UK sweetcorn season is right now, and this paella-style one-pan dish turns a cob of corn into a smoky, saffron-golden summer meal that's stupidly easy — no special pan, no faff. A great novelty weeknight dinner with proper Spanish depth from chorizo and smoked paprika.

Ingredients

  • 1 large ear of sweetcorn
  • 100g pancetta or streaky bacon
  • 1 medium onion
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 red pepper
  • 100g chorizo sausage
  • 600ml chicken stock
  • a few strands of saffron
  • 1 tbsp tomato puree
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp smoked hot paprika
  • 1/2 tsp cayenne pepper
  • 200g (1 cup) paella rice (Bomba or Calasparra)
  • salt and pepper

Instructions

Prep

  • Shuck the ear of corn, stand the cob vertically on a chopping board, and run a large sharp knife down all sides to cut the kernels off.
  • Dice the pancetta or streaky bacon.
  • Peel and chop the onion, peel and chop the garlic cloves.
  • Core and roughly chop the red pepper.
  • Slice the chorizo.
  • Heat the chicken stock (or dissolve a cube in boiling water), then stir in the saffron strands and the tomato puree so both dissolve.

Cook

  • Heat the vegetable oil in your largest pan (around 25cm) over medium heat and fry the pancetta for 2 minutes until just beginning to crisp.
  • Add the onion and garlic, season with salt and pepper, and fry without stirring for 2 minutes until the onion softens.
  • Add the red pepper and chorizo and cook together for 1 minute, letting the chorizo start to release its oil.
  • Stir in the sweetcorn kernels, then sprinkle over the smoked paprika and cayenne and stir to coat.
  • Pour in all the stock at once, turn up the heat, and let it bubble vigorously for 2-3 minutes.
  • Scatter the rice evenly over the surface — and from this point on, do NOT stir. Cook for 10 minutes until the rice shows through the liquid; gently nudge any dry grains that have stuck to the chorizo, but leave the bed undisturbed. If it looks too dry too soon, add a splash of water.
  • Turn the heat down, cover the pan with a lid (or a large baking sheet if no lid fits), and simmer for a further 10 minutes until all the liquid is absorbed.

Finish

  • Take the lid off and turn the heat right up for 1 minute until you can hear the rice start to pop and crackle — then keep it on for a final 30 seconds before taking the pan off the heat.
  • Cover the pan with a clean dry tea towel to absorb the steam and let it stand for 10 minutes.

Serve

  • Lift off the tea towel, give the pan a gentle shake, and serve straight from the pan with a plain green salad.

Pro Tips

  • Resist the urge to stir once the rice is in — leaving it undisturbed lets the grains toasty on the bottom and gives that paella 'socarrat' flavour that stirring would destroy.
  • Bomba or Calasparra rice is ideal because it holds its shape; in a pinch, short-grain risotto arborio works. For extra protein, nestle in a handful of peeled prawns in the last 10 minutes of simmering.
