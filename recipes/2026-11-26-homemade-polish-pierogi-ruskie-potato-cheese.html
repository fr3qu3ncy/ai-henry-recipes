Homemade Polish Pierogi Ruskie (Potato & Cheese)

Why This Recipe

Polish pierogi ruskie are peak midwinter comfort — tender hand-rolled dumplings stuffed with riced potato and salty farmer's cheese, boiled then served in nutty brown butter. A genuine technique project (dough, filling, rolling, sealing) from a cuisine not yet on the site, and the filling keeps well so you can make a batch and freeze half.

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 to 3/4 cup hot water (add as needed)
  • 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 3/4 lb (350g) russet potatoes, peeled
  • 1 medium onion, chopped
  • 1 tbsp ghee or butter
  • 7 oz (200g) farmer's cheese (or drained ricotta)
  • 1/2 to 1 tsp salt + black pepper, for the filling
  • Browned butter (about 3 tbsp butter) for serving
  • Sour cream or smetana, to serve (optional)

Instructions

Prep

  • In a large bowl combine the flour, 1 tsp salt and olive oil. Add 1/2 cup hot water and mix until a shaggy dough forms; add more hot water a little at a time (up to 3/4 cup) if it's too dry, or a dusting of flour if too sticky, until it just comes together.
  • Knead briefly until smooth, then cover with a kitchen towel and rest for 20–30 minutes. This relaxes the gluten so the dough rolls thin without springing back.
  • Meanwhile, peel the potatoes, cut into even chunks, cover with cold water in a pot, and bring to a boil. Cook about 20 minutes until easily pierced with a fork.
  • Heat ghee/butter in a medium pan over medium heat and cook the chopped onion 8–10 minutes, until soft and translucent.

Filling

  • Drain the potatoes well and pass them through a ricer (or mash very finely while still hot) to a smooth, dry texture — no lumps.
  • Stir the cooked onion and farmer's cheese into the potatoes. Season with 1/2–1 tsp salt and black pepper to taste.
  • Let the filling cool slightly so it's easy to handle, then cover and set aside.

Assemble

  • Divide the dough into 2 balls; cover one with a towel. Roll out the other on a lightly floured surface until about 1/8in (3mm) thick.
  • Cut into 3in (7.5cm) circles with a cookie cutter or glass.
  • Spoon ~1 tbsp of filling into each circle, dampen the edge, fold over into a half-moon, and press the edges firmly to seal. Place on a floured surface and cover with a towel to keep them from drying.

Cook & Serve

  • Bring a large pot of salted water to a rolling boil. Drop in the pierogi a few at a time (don't overcrowd) and cook 3–4 minutes, until they float to the top and plump up.
  • Remove with a slotted spoon and drain briefly on a clean tea towel.
  • For brown butter: melt 3 tbsp butter in a small pan over medium heat and cook, swirling, until it turns gold and smells nutty (about 2 min) — don't let it brown too far or it turns bitter.
  • Toss the pierogi with the brown butter, top with a spoonful of sour cream, and serve immediately.

Pro Tips

  • Rice the potatoes instead of mashing them for a smoother, drier filling — a wet filling tears the delicate dough when you seal it.
  • The pierogi freeze beautifully: lay the sealed, uncooked ones in a single layer on a parchment-lined tray, freeze until solid, then bag. Boil frozen pierogi straight from the freezer, adding 1–2 minutes to the cook time.
