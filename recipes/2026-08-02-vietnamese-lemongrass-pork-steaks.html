Vietnamese Lemongrass Pork Steaks

Why This Recipe

Vietnamese street food at home — pork shoulder tenderised with baking soda (a brilliant technique Martin hasn't tried yet), marinated in lemongrass, fish sauce, and lime, then seared in a screaming-hot skillet for authentic char-grill flavour. Completely different from his Korean dishes but shares those bold, aromatic qualities he loves.

Ingredients

  • 500g / 1 lb pork shoulder, skinless and boneless (or pork scotch roast)
  • 2 lemongrass stalks, white part only, bruised and cut into 1cm slices on the angle
  • 4 garlic cloves, finely minced
  • 4 tbsp lime juice
  • 3 tbsp fish sauce
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce (all-purpose or light, NOT dark soy)
  • 4 tbsp brown sugar, tightly packed
  • 3 tbsp canola oil (for marinade)
  • 3 tbsp oil for cooking (canola, vegetable, or peanut)
  • 3/4 tsp baking soda (for tenderising)

Instructions

Prep the Pork

  • Cut the pork shoulder into 8 equal, thin slices — about 1cm thick. A 45° angle cut works well if the shape is awkward.
  • Place each steak between two layers of cling wrap or inside a freezer bag. Pound with the jagged side of a meat mallet to 5mm / 0.2" thickness. This physically breaks down the tough fibres.
  • Set the pounded pork aside.

Marinate

  • In a medium bowl, combine the minced garlic, lime juice, fish sauce, soy sauce, brown sugar, 3 tbsp canola oil, and baking soda. The mixture will fizz when the baking soda hits the acid — this is normal.
  • Add the lemongrass slices to the marinade.
  • Add the pounded pork steaks and toss well with tongs until every piece is thoroughly coated.
  • Transfer everything (pork + marinade + lemongrass) to a ziplock bag. Squeeze out excess air and seal.
  • Refrigerate for 24 hours for maximum flavour penetration. The baking soda works during this time to chemically tenderise the meat, raising the pH so the proteins can't tighten up during cooking.

Pre-Cook

  • Remove pork from the marinade. Use your fingers or a spoon to brush off any lemongrass pieces clinging to the meat — these will burn in the hot pan.
  • Discard the marinade — it has done its job.

Cook

  • Heat a cast iron skillet or heavy-based frying pan over HIGH heat until you see wisps of smoke rising from the surface.
  • Add 1 1/2 tbsp cooking oil and swirl to coat the pan.
  • Cook the first batch of 4 pork steaks for 1 1/2 minutes on the first side — do not move them. They should develop a deep, dark char.
  • Flip and cook for 1 minute on the other side until charred and cooked through.
  • Transfer to a plate. Scrape the pan clean of any loose black bits with a spatula.
  • Add the remaining 1 1/2 tbsp oil and cook the second batch the same way: 1 1/2 minutes first side, 1 minute second side.

Serve

  • Serve immediately with jasmine rice and a simple side salad (shredded cucumber, carrot, and cabbage with a squeeze of lime).
  • For a full Vietnamese experience, serve in noodle bowls with rice vermicelli, fresh herbs (mint, coriander, Thai basil), and a dipping sauce of fish sauce, lime, sugar, garlic, and chilli.
  • Note: The pork will look pink inside — this is normal and caused by the baking soda. It is fully cooked and safe to eat.

Pro Tips

  • The baking soda tenderisation is the secret weapon — it raises the pH of the meat so the proteins can't contract and squeeze out moisture during cooking. The result: pork shoulder that's as tender as premium chops, even when cooked well done. Same technique used for velveting chicken and beef in Chinese stir-fries.
  • Marinate for the full 24 hours — the flavour penetration is transformative. If you're short on time, 4 hours still works, but overnight is where the magic happens. You can freeze the marinated pork for up to a month and defrost before cooking.
