Lomo Saltado (Peruvian Beef Stir-Fry)

Why This Recipe

Chinese-Peruvian chifa fusion at its finest — deeply seared beef, blistered onions and juicy tomatoes in a soy-oyster sauce, tossed with crispy French fries and served over rice. Wok-heat stir-fry technique in a brand-new cuisine territory.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb flank steak, cut against the grain into thin slices
  • 5 tbsp low-sodium soy sauce, divided
  • 5 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp oyster sauce
  • 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 1 tbsp aji amarillo paste (or gochujang, see tips)
  • 1 tsp cornstarch
  • 1 red onion, sliced into 1/2 inch wedges
  • 3 roma tomatoes, seeded and cut into 1/2 inch wedges
  • Oil for cooking
  • Sliced scallions and chopped cilantro for serving
  • Cooked white rice and French fries to serve

Instructions

Prep

  • Slice the flank steak thinly against the grain. Marinate with 1 tbsp soy sauce and half the garlic for 5-7 minutes (or 30 minutes with a pinch of baking soda if using a tougher cut).
  • Whisk the remaining 4 tbsp soy sauce with the oyster sauce, red wine vinegar, aji amarillo paste and cornstarch into a small bowl. Set aside.
  • Cut the red onion into 1/2-inch wedges and seed and wedge the tomatoes. Cook the rice and fry the potatoes so everything is ready to go.

Sear

  • Add about 1 tbsp oil to a cast-iron pan or wok over high heat until it shimmers.
  • Sear the beef in batches for 1-2 minutes, flipping to cook evenly — you want it seared, not steamed, so don't crowd the pan.
  • Transfer the cooked beef to a plate and keep warm.

Stir-fry

  • Add another tbsp of oil if the pan is dry, then add the onion wedges and let them blister for 1 minute.
  • Add the remaining garlic and the tomato wedges and sauté for a further minute until the tomatoes soften but still hold their shape.

Finish & Serve

  • Return the beef to the pan, pour over the whisked sauce and toss over high heat until it glazes and thickens, about 1 minute.
  • Serve immediately over rice with the French fries (tossed straight into the sauce if you like them saucy, on the side if you like them crispy).
  • Top with sliced scallions and chopped cilantro — both, ideally.

Pro Tips

  • No aji amarillo paste? A spoon of gochujang gives a similar fruity, smoky chilli depth — it's the closest pantry stand-in. For Martin's taste: moderate heat, so gochujang is a better match than raw chilli flakes.
  • The sear is everything: cook the beef in batches in a screaming-hot pan so it chars instead of stewing in its own juice. That 'wok hei' is what makes it taste restaurant-made.
