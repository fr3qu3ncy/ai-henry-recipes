Pot Roast Pheasant with Saffron Sweetcorn Mash

Why This Recipe

UK pheasant season just opened and this is the ideal way to cook a fresh bird — pot-roasting keeps the lean game meat deliciously moist, and the saffron sweetcorn mash is a brilliant autumn pairing. Mostly hands-off: one lid on, one hour of gentle cooking, and a rich cream-wine sauce to finish.

Ingredients

  • 1 pheasant (about 1kg), plucked, gutted and ready to cook (ask the butcher to prep it)
  • 15g butter
  • 1 tbsp sunflower oil
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 500g carrots, cut into batons
  • 4 sprigs fresh tarragon
  • 150ml dry Riesling
  • 100ml double cream
  • 1.2kg large baking potatoes
  • 45g butter, for the mash
  • 180g sweetcorn, cooked (from a jar)
  • 200-250ml whole milk
  • 1-2 generous pinches of saffron threads
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper

Instructions

Prep

  • Put the large baking potatoes in a baking tray and roast at 200C/180C Fan/Gas 6 for about 1 hour, until cooked right through — you'll need to scoop the flesh out later.
  • While the potatoes bake, bring 200ml of the milk to a boil in a small saucepan, remove from the heat, add the saffron threads and leave to infuse for as long as possible.

Cook

  • Place a large flameproof casserole (big enough to hold the pheasant and all the carrots) over a high heat. Add the 15g butter and the sunflower oil and melt together.
  • Add the pheasant and fry, turning regularly, until golden-brown all over (8-10 minutes). Remove and set aside.
  • Reduce the heat to medium and stir in the onion and sliced garlic. Fry gently for 3-4 minutes until tender.
  • Add the carrot batons and tarragon sprigs and stir around for 4-5 minutes.
  • Return the pheasant to the pot, nestling it breast-side down among the carrots.
  • Pour over the Riesling, season with salt and pepper, and bring to a boil.
  • Cover with a tight-fitting lid, turn the heat down low and cook gently for 1 hour, turning the pheasant over after 30 minutes.

Finish

  • Cut the baked potatoes in half and scoop the flesh into a clean saucepan. Add the 45g butter and a good pinch of salt, and place over a low heat.
  • Gradually add the saffron milk, mashing continuously. Stop and assess — add more milk if you like your mash softer and runnier.
  • Stir in the cooked sweetcorn and season to taste with salt and pepper.
  • Lift the pheasant out of the casserole onto a serving plate and keep warm. Check it's tender and cooked right through — a knife should slide into the thigh with no resistance.

Serve

  • Pour the double cream into the casserole with the cooking juices and carrots, and simmer for 2 minutes until the sauce is lightly thickened. Season to taste.
  • Spoon the cream sauce and carrots around the pheasant and serve the saffron sweetcorn mash alongside.

Pro Tips

  • Buy the pheasant 5-7 days ahead and let the butcher hang it — aged game is far more flavourful and the meat is less gamey. If it's very fresh, pot-roasting in wine keeps it moist even so.
  • Don't skimp on the saffron — 2 generous pinches makes a proper golden, nutty-coloured mash. A dry Riesling is key to the sauce; a sweet one would make the whole dish cloying.
