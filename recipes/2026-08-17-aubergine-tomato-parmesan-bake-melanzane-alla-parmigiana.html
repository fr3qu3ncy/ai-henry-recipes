Aubergine, Tomato & Parmesan Bake (Melanzane alla Parmigiana)

Why This Recipe

Mid-August is peak aubergine season, and this Italian classic is the perfect way to celebrate it — smoky griddled slices layered with simmered tomatoes and plenty of Parmesan. It's a genuinely hands-off oven bake that serves a crowd and is even better the next day.

Ingredients

  • 2 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 6 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 x 400g cans chopped tomatoes
  • 2 tbsp tomato purée
  • 4 aubergines, cut into long 5mm thick slices
  • 85g Parmesan (or vegetarian alternative), freshly grated
  • 20g pack basil, leaves torn
  • 1 egg, beaten

Instructions

Prep

  • Heat oven to 200C/fan 180C/gas 6.
  • Crush the garlic and tear the basil leaves.
  • Cut the aubergines into long, 5mm-thick slices and grate the Parmesan.

Cook

  • In a shallow pan, combine the garlic with 4 tbsp of the olive oil. Cook over a high heat for 3 minutes until fragrant.
  • Tip in the chopped tomatoes and simmer for 8 minutes, stirring now and then. Stir in the tomato purée and set aside.
  • Meanwhile, heat a griddle pan until very hot. Brush a few aubergine slices with a little oil and add to the pan.
  • Cook over a high heat for 5-7 minutes until well browned and cooked through, turning halfway through.
  • Lift onto kitchen paper and repeat with the remaining aubergine in batches — don't overcrowd the pan.

Assemble & Bake

  • Lay a few of the browned aubergine slices in the bottom of an ovenproof dish, then spoon over some of the tomato sauce.
  • Sprinkle with Parmesan and torn basil leaves, add a pinch of seasoning, then repeat the layers with the remaining ingredients.
  • Pour the beaten egg over the top, sprinkle over a little more Parmesan, and bake for 20 minutes or until the topping is golden.

Serve

  • Rest for 5 minutes out of the oven so it sets into neat slices.
  • Serve straight from the dish with a simple green salad or crusty bread for the juices.

Pro Tips

  • Don't crowd the griddle pan — aubergine releases moisture fast, so cooking in batches is what gets you that proper char instead of steamed slices.
  • This bake is even better a day ahead: the layers settle and the flavours meld, so it's a brilliant make-ahead option for entertaining.
