Spiced Pork Shoulder with Celeriac, Leeks & Quick-Pickled Apple

Why This Recipe

Peak-season celeriac and leeks meet a fennel-and-spice rubbed pork shoulder in one big, mostly hands-off roast — the sort of slow oven dish Martin loves, with the quick-pickled apple adding a sharp, fennel-bright counterpoint.

Ingredients

  • 1 bone-in, skin-on British free-range pork shoulder (approx. 3.5kg), skin scored for crackling
  • 1 garlic bulb, halved horizontally
  • 1 litre fresh chicken stock
  • 1 large or 2 small celeriac, peeled and chopped into 3cm pieces
  • 6 leeks, chopped into 4 large equal lengths
  • 1 tbsp fennel seeds
  • 1 tsp coriander seeds
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 1 tsp sweet smoked paprika
  • 1/2 tsp English mustard powder
  • 1 tsp ground ginger
  • 2 tbsp soft brown sugar
  • 1.5 tbsp salt
  • 3 tbsp rapeseed oil
  • 40ml cider vinegar
  • 40g caster sugar
  • 1 large or 2 small Granny Smith apples, cored and thinly sliced
  • 1 tbsp fennel seeds (for the pickled apple)

Instructions

Prep

  • Toast the fennel, coriander and cumin seeds in a dry frying pan over medium heat for about 2 minutes, tossing, until fragrant.
  • Pound the toasted seeds in a pestle and mortar (or whizz in a spice grinder) to a powder.
  • Combine with the smoked paprika, English mustard powder, ground ginger, soft brown sugar, salt and rapeseed oil to make the spice rub.
  • Thoroughly rub the spice mix into the skin and meat of the pork shoulder, then rest it uncovered for 2-3 hours so it comes to room temperature.
  • Meanwhile, for the quick-pickled apple: stir the cider vinegar and caster sugar together in a bowl until the sugar dissolves.
  • Add the thinly sliced apple and the fennel seeds, toss well, cover and chill in the fridge until ready to serve.

Cook

  • Heat the oven to 240C (220C fan/gas 9). Line a large roasting tin with foil and place it in the bottom of the oven.
  • Set the spice-rubbed pork on an oven rack in the middle of the oven, over the foil-lined tin, and roast for 30 minutes until the skin has started to puff up and crackle.
  • Lower the oven to 170C (150C fan/gas 2) and cook for a further 3 to 3.5 hours.
  • After 1 hour at the lower temperature, remove the foil from the tray and add the garlic bulb halves, half the stock, and the celeriac and leeks around the pork.
  • After another hour (2 hours in total), add the remaining stock to the tin.
  • After 3 hours, check the meat with a digital probe thermometer — it should read about 70-75C near the bone.

Serve

  • Take the pork out of the oven and switch the oven off, leaving the vegetables in the roasting tin to keep warm — they should look dark golden and sticky.
  • Rest the pork for at least 20 minutes before carving.
  • Carve the pork and serve with the roasted celeriac and leeks, the quick-pickled apple, and the pan juices drizzled over.

Pro Tips

  • Have your butcher score the pork skin for you — deep, even scores that cut through to the fat (not the meat) are the difference between proper crackling and a soggy top.
  • The 2-3 hour rest is not optional: a cold shoulder straight into a hot oven will undercook the centre before the crackling sets. Start the rub as soon as you get home and it'll be ready when you are.
