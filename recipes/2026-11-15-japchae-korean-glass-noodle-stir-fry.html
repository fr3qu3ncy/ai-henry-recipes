Japchae (Korean Glass Noodle Stir-Fry)

Why This Recipe

Korean cuisine is where you've been experimenting lately, and japchae is the classic you haven't hit yet — sweet potato glass noodles with a chewy bite, coated in a glossy soy-sesame sauce with crisp stir-fried veg. It's the royal-court dish behind every Korean noodle takeout order, and it holds well for days, so it's also perfect as a meal-prep lunch.

Ingredients

  • 8 oz Korean sweet potato glass noodles (dangmyeon)
  • 1 tbsp sesame oil (plus extra for finishing)
  • 1 small onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 carrot, julienned
  • 1 red bell pepper, sliced
  • 2 cups spinach
  • 1 cup shiitake or oyster mushrooms, sliced
  • 2 green onions, chopped
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil, for stir-frying
  • Sauce: 3 tbsp soy sauce
  • Sauce: 2 tbsp sugar
  • Sauce: 1 tbsp sesame oil
  • Sauce: 1 tsp garlic, minced
  • Sauce: 1 tsp rice vinegar (optional)
  • Sauce: 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • To finish: sesame seeds, extra green onion

Instructions

Prep

  • Slice every vegetable thinly so they cook fast and evenly — onion, carrot, pepper, mushrooms; keep the spinach and green onions raw for later.
  • Whisk the sauce together in a small bowl: soy sauce, sugar, sesame oil, minced garlic, rice vinegar and black pepper. Taste — it should taste slightly strong on its own, because the noodles will dilute it.

Cook

  • Boil the glass noodles in plenty of water for 6-7 minutes until chewy, not soft. They should still have a springy bite.
  • Drain and rinse under cold water, then toss with 1 tbsp sesame oil and a little soy sauce so they don't stick.
  • Heat 1 tbsp neutral oil in a wok or large pan over high heat. Stir-fry the onion for 2-3 minutes until just tender, transfer to a plate.
  • Add a little more oil and stir-fry the carrot and red pepper for 3-4 minutes until crisp-tender but still bright. Transfer out.
  • Stir-fry the mushrooms for 2-3 minutes until their edges just start to brown. Transfer out.
  • Wipe the pan if needed, add the spinach with a splash of water and toss for 30-60 seconds just until wilted. Transfer out.
  • Return all the cooked vegetables to the pan with the noodles over medium-high heat.
  • Pour in the prepared sauce and toss for 1-2 minutes until every strand is glossy and evenly coated — don't rush this.

Finish

  • Turn off the heat and drizzle a final little extra sesame oil over the top — this is what gives japchae its signature aroma.
  • Cut the noodle strands with scissors to an easy eating length, then plate into wide bowls.
  • Top with sesame seeds and chopped green onion.

Serve

  • Serve warm or at room temperature. It keeps in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4 days — reheat gently with a splash of water to loosen the noodles.

Pro Tips

  • Cook each vegetable separately — it feels like extra work, but tossing everything into one pan turns japchae into a watery, soggy mess. Clean texture is the whole point.
  • The glass noodles should stay chewy (dangmyeon's signature bite). If they go soft and translucent-pale they're overdone — and the dish's texture changes completely. A splash more soy at the end keeps it glossy and slightly saucy.
