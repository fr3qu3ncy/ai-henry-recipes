German Braised Red Cabbage (Rotkohl)

Why This Recipe

Midwinter is exactly when red cabbage tastes sweetest, and this is the classic German answer to it — a slow braise with apple, vinegar and juniper that turns a humble, cheap vegetable into a jewel-toned sweet-and-sour side that would sit proudly beside roast pork or duck at Christmas.

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 1 medium head red cabbage, cored and finely shredded
  • 1 Granny Smith apple, deseeded and grated
  • 1 medium yellow onion, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 cup (120ml) apple cider or red wine vinegar
  • 1/3 cup (65g) granulated white sugar
  • 1/2 cup (120ml) water
  • 3-4 dried juniper berries
  • Pinch of ground cloves

Instructions

Prep

  • Core the red cabbage and slice it very finely — thin ribbons braise faster and absorb the sweet-sour liquid evenly.
  • Grate the apple (keep the peel for colour), slice the onion thin.
  • Crush the juniper berries lightly with the flat of a knife so their oils release into the braise.

Cook

  • Melt the butter in a 6-quart (or larger) lidded pot over medium heat.
  • Add the cabbage, apple, onion, vinegar, sugar, water, juniper berries and cloves.
  • Bring to a boil over medium heat, then turn the heat down to low and cover.
  • Simmer for about 1 hour, stirring every 15 minutes, until the cabbage is tender and turning translucent.

Serve

  • Taste and adjust — a pinch more sugar if it is too sharp, a splash more vinegar if too sweet.
  • Spoon onto plates with roast pork, duck, sausages or any winter meat.

Pro Tips

  • Swap the apple cider vinegar for red wine (or half and half) for a deeper, more savoury braise — very traditional.
  • Rotkohl keeps well in the fridge for several days and tastes even better next day as the flavours marry — make a big batch and use it to finish off Christmas leftovers.
