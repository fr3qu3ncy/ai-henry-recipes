Caldereta de Pescado (Catalan Fish Stew)

Why This Recipe

A soul-warming Catalan fish stew that puts saffron, firm white fish and shellfish in one pot with a clever picada - a toasted almond, bread and garlic paste that thickens the broth. Peak autumn seafood, and the picada technique is one you will use in every stew from now on.

Ingredients

  • 4 tbsp extra virgin olive oil (plus more for drizzling)
  • 60g (1/4 cup) almonds
  • 1/2 slice day-old white bread, torn up
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 small onion, chopped
  • 2 very ripe large tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 red chilli, deseeded and diced
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 1 litre fish stock
  • 2 medium potatoes, peeled and sliced 1cm thick
  • 20 saffron threads
  • 400g firm-fleshed fish (hake, sea bream), cut into steaks
  • 8 raw prawns, shells on
  • 300g clams in shells, scrubbed clean
  • Handful of chopped flat-leaf parsley (or cilantro for a Portuguese twist)

Instructions

Prep

  • Heat the olive oil in a large saucepan over medium heat. Add the almonds, torn bread and garlic and cook until everything is golden, 4-5 minutes.
  • Remove the solids from the pan, leaving as much of the toasted oil behind as possible. Pound in a pestle and mortar (or blitz in a food processor) into a chunky paste - this is the picada.

Cook

  • In the same pan, saute the chopped onion for about 5 minutes until soft. Stir in the chopped tomatoes and let the juices evaporate for 2-3 minutes.
  • Add the diced chilli, bay leaf and fish stock. Bring to a boil, then add the potato slices and saffron threads.
  • Simmer gently for about 10 minutes, until the potatoes are just tender.
  • Stir in the picada and mix well so it dissolves into the broth, thickening it slightly.

Finish

  • Five minutes before serving, add the fish first, largest steaks first, and cook for 2-3 minutes until just opaque.
  • Add the prawns and cook for about 1 minute, then add the clams and simmer for 1 minute until they open. Discard any clams that stay shut.

Serve

  • Ladle into deep bowls, sprinkle with chopped parsley and finish with a good drizzle of extra virgin olive oil. Eat immediately with bread.

Pro Tips

  • The picada is the whole trick - toasted nuts plus day-old bread is the Catalan way of thickening stews, and the same idea appears in Spanish cocido. Make it a little coarser than smooth for texture.
  • Time the shellfish strictly: fish in first, then prawns, then clams last. Overcooked clams turn rubbery and prawns curl up tight - the one-minute stages keep everything at peak tenderness. Soak the clams in cold salted water for 20 minutes beforehand to purge any sand.
