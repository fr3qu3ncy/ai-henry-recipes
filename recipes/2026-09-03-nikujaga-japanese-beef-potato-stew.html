Nikujaga (Japanese Beef & Potato Stew)

Why This Recipe

Classic Japanese home-cooking at its coziest — perfect as autumn settles in. The real lesson here is technique: rangiri diagonal cuts, mentori potato shaping, and an otoshibuta drop lid that stops everything from breaking apart. Tastes even better the next day, so it's a great make-ahead.

Ingredients

  • 1 onion (250g), cut into 1/2-inch wedges
  • 1 carrot (125g), rangiri diagonal-cut into 1-inch pieces
  • 3 Yukon Gold potatoes (545g), quartered with sharp edges shaved off (mentori)
  • 8 snow peas, strings removed
  • 1 pack shirataki noodles (200g)
  • 225g (1/2 lb) thinly sliced beef (ribeye or beef steak for stew)
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • 2 cups (480ml) dashi (or light stock/broth)
  • 4 tbsp mirin
  • 4 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp sake
  • 1 tbsp sugar

Instructions

Prep

  • Cut the onion in half, then each half into 1/2-inch (1.3cm) wedges.
  • Rangiri-cut the carrot: slice diagonally, rotating a quarter turn between cuts, into 1-inch pieces — more surface area means faster cooking and more flavour absorption.
  • Quarter the potatoes, then shave off the sharp edges with a knife (mentori technique) so they won't break apart in the simmer.
  • Soak the potatoes in cold water for a few minutes to remove starch, then drain.
  • Blanch the snow peas in boiling salted water for 1 minute; keep the water boiling.
  • Drain the shirataki noodles, cut roughly in half, and blanch for 1 minute in the same pot to remove the raw odor; drain well.
  • Cut the thinly sliced beef into pieces about 3 inches (7.6cm) wide.

Cook

  • Heat a large pot or Dutch oven over medium heat, add the oil, and sauté the onion wedges until coated.
  • Add the beef and cook until no longer pink.
  • Add the potatoes and coat them well in the pan juices — this coating helps them hold their shape.
  • Add the carrot and shirataki noodles, and stir to combine.
  • Pour in the dashi — enough to almost (not fully) cover the ingredients. Top up with water if needed.
  • Bring to a boil, then skim off the scum and foam from the surface with a skimmer or spoon.
  • Stir in the sugar, sake, soy sauce, and mirin. If you have an otoshibuta (circular drop lid), place it on top; otherwise a piece of parchment cut to the pot's diameter works.
  • Cover and simmer on low for 12-14 minutes, until a skewer slides easily into the potatoes. Do NOT stir — the drop lid keeps everything in place and distributes the liquid.

Rest & Serve

  • Turn off the heat, remove the drop lid, and let the stew rest uncovered for 30-60 minutes — the flavours soak in as it cools. (It's excellent the next day too; refrigerates 3-4 days.)
  • When ready to serve, add the snow peas to the pot, cover, and reheat over medium until simmering, then simmer a couple of minutes so they keep their bright green colour.
  • Serve in bowls with plenty of the cooking liquid. Eat with rice and pickles if you can.

Pro Tips

  • The otoshibuta (or parchment circle) is the secret weapon — it stops the vegetables from colliding and breaking apart, so you never need to stir. If you don't have one, a circle of baking parchment fitted to the pot's inner diameter does the job.
  • Nikujaga is a 'next day is better' dish — the potatoes and beef drink up the dashi-soy-mirin sauce as they cool. If you're not eating it straight away, let it rest the full hour, then gently reheat before serving.
