Seolleongtang (Korean Beef Bone Soup)

Why This Recipe

Korea's quintessential winter soup — milky-white beef bone broth that only turns that colour if you boil the bones hard enough, a genuine technique to learn. The big-boil, low-sodium broth with sliced beef is Korea's answer to a cold midwinter night, and it makes a huge pot from humble bones.

Ingredients

  • 5 lbs beef bones (marrow, femur, knuckle — a mix is best)
  • 22 quarts (5.5 gallons) water, added in stages
  • 1–2 lb beef brisket or flank steak, for the topping
  • 10 cups water (for the brisket)
  • 1/2 onion, for the brisket
  • 1 green onion, for the brisket
  • 1 piece radish (optional, for the brisket)
  • 2 green onions, chopped, for serving
  • Korean sea salt (cheonilyeom) or flaky sea salt, to taste
  • Black pepper, to taste
  • Optional: somyeon (thin Korean noodles) or cooked white rice to stir into the bowl

Instructions

Prep

  • Soak the beef bones in a big bowl of cold water for 30 minutes — some blood will leach out and the water will turn pink. Drain.
  • Add the bones to a large stockpot (10 qt or bigger) with 4 quarts of water. Cover and bring to a boil, then boil for 15 minutes.

Blanch

  • Turn off the heat and discard that murky liquid — save the bones.
  • Rinse the bones quickly under running water to clear off the brown foamy impurities, and give the pot a rinse.
  • Return the rinsed bones to the pot, add 4 more quarts of water, cover, and bring to a full rolling boil.

The Hard Boil (this makes the broth white)

  • Boil hard for 30 minutes, then top up with 4 cups of water. Keep boiling at medium to just-above-medium for 1 hour, adding another 4 cups of water so the water level sits just above the bones, and boil for a further hour or so.
  • The broth must boil vigorously during this stage — the bones knocking around is what emulsifies the fat into that cloudy, milky colour. If the water evaporates too low, top up a little.

The Long Simmer

  • Once the broth is opaque and milky, add about 8 quarts of water and drop the heat to medium-low. Keep a full simmer for 3 hours.
  • Add the remaining ~7 quarts of water and keep a full simmer for another 4.5 hours, until the broth is completely opaque, milky and deeply savoury. Skim any excess fat as it collects.
  • Meanwhile, cook the topping beef: in a separate 3–4 qt pot, add the brisket/flank with 10 cups of water, half an onion, a green onion and a piece of radish. Bring to a boil, then simmer about 1 hour until a fork slides in easily. Cool slightly, then slice the beef very thinly across the grain.
  • If there's a thick layer of fat on top, the easiest fix is to cool the pot (or just skim) and lift it off with a spoon. Or swirl the base of your ladle across the surface before each ladle to leave the fat behind.

Serve

  • Ladle the boiling broth into bowls with a few thin slices of beef and, if you like, a slice of radish.
  • Season each bowl at the table with salt and a generous grind of black pepper, then top with chopped green onion.
  • Serve with warm cooked rice or thin somyeon noodles — stir them straight into the soup for a thick, porridge-like bowl, plus kimchi on the side.

Pro Tips

  • The milky colour is a technique, not an accident: water just enough to cover the bones, boiled hard at first. Add too much water too early and the broth stays thin and clear — if that happens, drain some off and keep boiling hard until it clouds up.
  • You can re-boil the same bones a second (even third) time with fresh water for another 5 hours — many Korean shops do, and some say the second broth is even better. It's also how you scale the pot if you only have a smaller one.
