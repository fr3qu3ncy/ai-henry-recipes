Maqluba with Lamb (Palestinian Upside-Down Rice)

Why This Recipe

A showstopping one-pot Levantine dish where layered rice, spiced lamb and summer vegetables are flipped out onto a platter like a savoury cake — dinner and theatre in one. Peak aubergine, courgette and tomato season makes this the perfect showstopper, and it cooks hands-off once layered.

Ingredients

  • 500 g basmati rice
  • 2 tbsp olive oil + a little more for greasing
  • 500 g minced lamb (or beef)
  • 1 tsp salt
  • Freshly ground black pepper
  • 800 ml hot chicken stock
  • 1 large onion, finely diced
  • 1 large aubergine, cut into rough cubes
  • 1 courgette, cut into rough cubes
  • 1 red capsicum (bell pepper), cut into rough cubes
  • 4 tomatoes, sliced into rounds
  • 1 small cauliflower, cut into bite-sized pieces
  • 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil (for the cauliflower)
  • 2 tsp allspice
  • 1/2 tsp turmeric
  • 30 g pine nuts, for serving
  • 1 handful fresh parsley, chopped, for serving

Instructions

Prep

  • Preheat the oven to 180°C (160°C fan).
  • Rinse the basmati rice until the water runs clear, then drain and set aside.
  • Finely dice the onion and cut the aubergine, courgette and red capsicum into rough cube shapes.
  • Slice the tomatoes into rounds and cut the cauliflower into bite-sized pieces.

Roast the Cauliflower

  • Toss the cauliflower pieces on a baking sheet with 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil, 1/4 tsp salt and a dash of black pepper.
  • Roast for about 20 minutes until the edges start to caramalise, flipping halfway through.

Brown the Lamb

  • Heat 2 tbsp olive oil in a large frying pan over medium heat.
  • Sauté the diced onion for 3-4 minutes until soft.
  • Add the minced lamb with the allspice, turmeric, salt and a grind of black pepper.
  • Stir and brown the meat all over, cooking for about 10 minutes until a little on the dry side but still holding some moisture.

Assemble & Cook

  • Generously grease a 20-22 cm saucepan (about 10 cm high) and line the bottom with the sliced tomatoes, building 2 layers. Season lightly with salt and pepper.
  • Add half the cooked lamb, season, and pack it down firmly with a potato masher.
  • Add all the roasted cauliflower and cubed vegetables, season, and pack down.
  • Top with half the rinsed rice, season, and flatten. Add the remaining lamb, season and pack down, then finish with the remaining rice, season and pack down firmly.
  • Place a small saucer face-down on the rice (or use the back of a large spoon) and slowly pour the hot stock over it — this stops craters forming in the rice.
  • Bring to a simmer over high heat for 3 minutes until you see the edges bubbling.
  • Put the lid on, turn the heat right down and cook for 45 minutes, adding 5-10 more minutes if the rice needs it.
  • Remove from the heat and let it rest for 10 minutes — this is the setting time that makes the flip clean.

Serve

  • Place a large serving platter over the pot, flip the whole thing over, and gently ease the saucepan off the plate.
  • Top with the pine nuts and chopped parsley.
  • Serve with plain yoghurt or a mint yoghurt sauce and a simple chopped salad of tomato, cucumber, parsley, lemon and olive oil. (If it does collapse, no big deal — scatter with the nuts and parsley and serve as is.)

Pro Tips

  • The 10-minute rest after cooking is non-negotiable — it sets the layers so the flip comes out clean and intact instead of collapsing.
  • Pack the pot down firmly and use hot stock: a tightly packed pot gives a tightly packed result, and hot stock keeps the rice cooking evenly. The saucer trick when pouring the stock prevents craters from breaking up your layers.
  • Aged basmati rice wants to be soaked briefly before cooking for the fluffiest, separate grains — a cheap trick for a restaurant finish.
