Briam: Traditional Greek Roasted Vegetables

Why This Recipe

The classic Greek roasted-vegetable tray that's the natural side to moussaka. Thin-sliced potato, courgette and red onion layered in a pan of canned tomatoes, seasoned with oregano, rosemary and garlic, then baked until soft, charred and all the juices have reduced to a sweet, saucy glaze. Vegetarian, hands-off, and serves a crowd.

Ingredients

  • 570g (1¼ lb) golden potatoes, peeled, thinly sliced into 5mm rounds
  • 570g (1¼ lb) courgette (2–3), thinly sliced into 6mm rounds
  • 1 large red onion (or 2 small), thinly sliced into rounds
  • salt and pepper
  • 2 tsp dried oregano
  • scant 1 tsp dried rosemary
  • 35g (½ cup) chopped fresh parsley
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • Greek extra virgin olive oil, plus extra for serving
  • 794g (28 oz) can diced tomatoes with juice (no-salt-added organic recommended)

Instructions

Prep

  • Preheat the oven to 200C/180 fan/gas 6 and place a rack in the middle.
  • Put the sliced potatoes and courgette in a large mixing bowl. Season with salt, pepper, oregano and rosemary.
  • Add the parsley, minced garlic and a generous drizzle of extra virgin olive oil, and toss until the vegetables are well coated.

Assemble

  • Pour half the canned tomatoes into a large round oven-safe pan (an 11-inch/28cm pan) and spread them over the bottom.
  • Arrange the seasoned potatoes, courgette and sliced onion in rows around the pan, alternating the vegetables.
  • Pour any remaining oil and garlic mixture over the vegetables, then top with the rest of the diced tomatoes.

Bake

  • Tent the pan loosely with foil so it isn't touching the vegetables, and bake for 45 minutes.
  • Remove the foil, then roast uncovered for a further 30-40 minutes, until the vegetables are soft and charred and most of the liquid has evaporated. Ovens vary, so check as you go.

Serve

  • Remove from the oven and serve warm or at room temperature with a generous final drizzle of extra virgin olive oil.

Pro Tips

  • Slice the potato thin (5mm) so it cooks through in the same time as the courgette - uneven thickness is what makes one tray of briam soggy and another undercooked.
  • The uncovered second half of the bake is where the flavour comes from: the tomato sauce must reduce and caramelise. If it's still swimming at the end, give it 10 more minutes.
