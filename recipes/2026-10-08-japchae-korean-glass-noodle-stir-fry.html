Japchae (Korean Glass Noodle Stir Fry)

Why This Recipe

Sweet potato glass noodles are a technique you rarely get outside Korea — and this is the authentic holiday-party method where every vegetable is sautéed separately for maximum flavour. A proper showpiece dish that's more technique than effort.

Ingredients

  • 3.5 tbsp canola or vegetable oil
  • 1/2 carrot, julienned (about 1 cup)
  • 1/2 onion, sliced thin
  • 1 bunch spinach
  • 8 oz (200g) Korean sweet potato glass noodles (dangmyeon)
  • 3 dried shiitake mushrooms, rehydrated and stemmed
  • 1/4 cup dried wood ear mushrooms (optional), rehydrated
  • 5 oz beef stew meat, cut into thin strips against the grain
  • Marinade: 2 tsp soy sauce, 2 tsp sugar, 1 tsp sesame oil, 1 tsp rice cooking wine, 1/2 tsp garlic powder
  • Noodle seasoning: 2 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp sugar, 1.5 tbsp sesame oil
  • Finishing sauce: 1.5-2 tbsp soy sauce (Jin Ganjang if you have it), 2 tsp sugar, 2 tsp sesame oil
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds
  • Salt to taste

Instructions

Prep

  • Cut the beef into thin strips against the grain. Mix with the marinade ingredients in a bowl and let sit for at least 10 minutes while you prep the rest.
  • Soak the dried shiitake and wood ear mushrooms in warm water for 10-20 minutes until fully plump. Rub each under cold running water, then cut shiitake into thin strips and wood ear into wide strips.
  • Julienne the carrot and slice the onion thin. Set both aside.
  • Toast the sesame seeds in a dry pan over low heat for 1-2 minutes until fragrant. Set aside.

Cook

  • Boil 8-10 cups of water in a large pot. Cook the glass noodles per package instructions (about 6 min) until completely clear and soft all the way to the center of each noodle — this is the key texture check. Drain well.
  • While the noodles are still warm, season them with 2 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp sugar and 1.5 tbsp sesame oil. Mix until glossy. Snip the noodles with scissors a few times so they're easier to eat.
  • Blanch the spinach: boil 6 cups of water with 1 tsp salt, drop in the spinach for no more than 40 seconds — it should still be a bit chewy. Shock in ice water, drain, and squeeze out excess water. Toss with 1/2 tsp salt and 1 tsp sesame oil.
  • Sauté each ingredient separately in the same pan (wipe it with a paper towel between each — don't let flavours cross): add 1 tbsp oil, cook the onions until transparent but not browned, with a pinch of salt. Remove.
  • Add 1 tbsp oil, sauté the carrots until soft and tender with a pinch of salt. Remove.
  • Add 1 tbsp oil, sauté the mushrooms with a pinch of salt for about 3 minutes. Remove.
  • Add 1/2 tbsp oil over medium-high heat. Sauté the beef until fully cooked — if any juices remain, keep cooking until they evaporate. Remove and let everything cool.

Finish

  • In a large bowl, combine the seasoned noodles, spinach, sautéed vegetables and beef. Toss well so everything is evenly distributed.
  • Add the finishing sauce (1.5-2 tbsp soy sauce, 2 tsp sugar, 2 tsp sesame oil) and toss again — don't skip this, it's what makes japchae taste properly glazed and glossy.

Serve

  • Sprinkle with the toasted sesame seeds and serve at room temperature or warm. Adjust with more soy sauce and sugar to taste.

Pro Tips

  • The separate-sauté method takes 10 extra minutes but is the authentic difference — each vegetable keeps its own flavour and the dish has proper texture layers instead of a mushy stir-fry. Wipe the pan, don't add more oil to the residue.
  • Glass noodles are only 'done' when the very center of each strand is clear — undercooked ones stay chewy-rubbery for hours and there's no second chance. When in doubt, cook 1 minute longer.
