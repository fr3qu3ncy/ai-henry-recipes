Oden: Japanese Fishcake & Daikon Winter Stew

Why This Recipe

Oden is THE signature Japanese winter dish — a konbini staple that hits its peak season right now. Slow-simmered in a deeply flavored dashi broth, it's all about patience and layering, a completely new technique for the kitchen.

Ingredients

  • 10g kombu seaweed
  • 30g katsuobushi (dried bonito flakes)
  • 1.5L water
  • 2 eggs
  • 4 pieces daikon, 2-3cm thick rounds
  • 200g konnyaku (konjac), from the fridge
  • 4 pieces soft fishcake (hanpen)
  • 4 pieces satsuma-age (deep-fried fish paste)
  • 4 pieces ganmodoki (deep-fried vegetable tofu)
  • 1 piece dried mochi (rice cake)
  • 1 piece abura-age (deep-fried thinly-sliced tofu)
  • 45ml soy sauce
  • 45ml mirin
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • Salt, to taste
  • Karashi mustard paste, for serving
  • Yuzu kosho, to serve (optional)

Instructions

Prep

  • Boil the eggs in water for 10 minutes for a fully hard-boiled egg, then cool and peel.
  • Make the dashi: warm 1.5L water with the kombu seaweed, remove the kombu just before it boils, add the katsuobushi flakes, then simmer for 5-6 minutes to deepen the flavour. Strain.
  • Peel the daikon and score a cross-cut on the surface of each piece so it absorbs the broth more easily.
  • Boil the daikon in a pot of water with a pinch of rice (or leftover rice water) from a cold start for about 15 minutes until just softened. Drain, rinse and dry on paper towels — this removes the raw daikon smell.
  • Score the surface of the konnyaku and cut it into 4 triangle shapes.
  • Make mochi kinchaku: halve both the dried mochi and the abura-age tofu pouch, open the tofu cut side without tearing, tuck a mochi half inside and secure with a toothpick.

Cook

  • Combine the dashi broth with the soy sauce, mirin and sugar in a large pot. Simmer briefly, then season with a little salt to taste.
  • Add the boiled eggs, daikon and konnyaku first — these take longest. Simmer gently over low heat for 30 minutes.
  • Add the satsuma-age, ganmodoki and mochi kinchaku and simmer for another 15 minutes.
  • Finally add the soft hanpen fishcake and simmer for just 1 minute so it stays pillowy.
  • If you have time, cool the pot and let it rest for half a day or overnight — the flavours deepen dramatically.

Serve

  • Ladle the warm oden into bowls with plenty of broth.
  • Serve with karashi mustard paste on the side, plus yuzu kosho if you have it.
  • Eat the daikon last — after soaking in the broth, it tastes as good as the fishcakes.

Pro Tips

  • The daikon par-boil in rice water is non-negotiable — it strips the raw, peppery bite so the daikon drinks up the dashi instead of fighting it.
  • Oden keeps and improves: leftovers re-warmed in the broth next day are genuinely better. Make a bigger pot and eat it over two sittings.
