Fabada Asturiana: Spanish White Bean & Chorizo Stew

Why This Recipe

Asturias' legendary winter bean stew is the perfect one-pot for late September — mostly hands-off simmering, with pancetta, chorizo and morcilla melting into a creamy paprika-kissed bean soup. A proper cold-weather classic that's dead simple to make.

Ingredients

  • 400g cooked white beans (canned or pre-soaked dried faba beans)
  • 600ml chicken stock
  • 4 chorizo sausages (about 150g)
  • 2 blood sausages / morcilla (about 100g)
  • 100g pancetta, thick-cut
  • 150g pork belly
  • 1 whole garlic head, outer skin removed
  • 1 whole onion, brown skin removed
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • a pinch of saffron threads
  • 120ml (1/2 cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • sea salt, to taste

Instructions

Prep

  • If using dried beans, soak them overnight in cold water and drain before starting (canned beans can go straight in).
  • Trim the pork belly into chunks and slice the pancetta thickly. Leave the chorizo and blood sausage whole.

Simmer

  • Add the garlic head, onion, beans, chicken stock, pork belly, pancetta, chorizo and blood sausage to a large pot and bring to a boil.
  • Boil for 1-2 minutes, skimming off any foam that rises (just the fat reducing).
  • Turn the heat to low and once gently simmering, drizzle in the olive oil, add the smoked paprika and saffron.
  • Simmer uncovered for 1-1.5 hours (canned beans) or 2+ hours (dried beans), topping up with a splash of cold water every 20 minutes to keep the beans covered.

Finish

  • Remove the meats and sausages with a slotted spoon and let them cool slightly, then slice thickly. They should be very tender.
  • Discard the onion and garlic — or, as the chef suggests, squeeze the cooked garlic cloves onto fresh bread and smear. Worth it.
  • Check the stew consistency: it should be slightly creamy. If too watery, take out a handful of beans, mash them and stir back in to thicken.
  • Season with a pinch of salt if needed.

Serve

  • Ladle the beans and their liquid into deep bowls.
  • Top each with slices of chorizo, a chunk of morcilla and pieces of pork belly and pancetta.
  • Serve piping hot with plenty of crusty bread for mopping.

Pro Tips

  • Mashing a few beans back into the pot is the traditional trick — it thickens the broth into that signature creamy texture without any thickener.
  • The whole garlic head and onion act as flavour bombs for the pot — don't eat them, but absolutely squeeze the garlic onto bread with olive oil. The traditionalists discard them; you should not.
