Doenjang Pasta (Korean Soybean Paste Spaghetti)

Why This Recipe

Korean-Italian fusion that swaps cream for doenjang (fermented soybean paste) — deeply umami, ready in 15 minutes of active time, and a novel twist on weeknight pasta Martin hasn't tried yet.

Ingredients

  • 6 oz / 180g spaghetti (or long pasta of choice)
  • 1-1/2 tbsp Korean soybean paste (doenjang)
  • 3 tbsp butter
  • 6 cloves garlic, sliced
  • 1/2 tsp chili flakes
  • 9 oz / 250g assorted mushrooms (shiitake, oyster, enoki)
  • 1/4 cup / 60ml white wine
  • Salt and pepper to taste (if needed)
  • Parmesan cheese, freshly grated (optional)
  • Scallions or chili flakes for garnish

Instructions

Prep

  • Slice the garlic cloves thinly.
  • Clean and prepare the mushrooms — tear oyster/enoki by hand, slice shiitake stems removed.
  • Whisk together the doenjang with 2-3 tablespoons of warm water in a small bowl until smooth and lump-free.

Cook Pasta

  • Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a boil.
  • Cook the spaghetti until al dente (usually 8-10 minutes, check package instructions).
  • Before draining, reserve 1/2 cup of the starchy pasta cooking water.
  • Drain the pasta and set aside.

Build the Sauce

  • In a large skillet, melt the butter over medium heat.
  • Add the sliced garlic and chili flakes, cook gently for about 1 minute until fragrant — do not let the garlic brown or burn.
  • Add the mushrooms and sauté for 2-3 minutes until softened but still meaty in texture.
  • Pour in the white wine and let it bubble for 30 seconds to evaporate the alcohol, scraping up any browned bits from the pan bottom.

Combine

  • Pour the loosened doenjang mixture into the skillet and stir until it reduces slightly and coats the mushrooms.
  • Add the drained pasta to the skillet and toss to combine.
  • Add reserved pasta water a splash at a time as needed to help the sauce cling to the noodles and achieve a silky consistency.

Serve

  • Taste and adjust seasoning if needed — doenjang is salty so you may not need extra salt.
  • Transfer to plates and finish with a light grating of Parmesan cheese.
  • Garnish with sliced scallions or a pinch of chili flakes.
  • Serve immediately while hot.

Pro Tips

  • Save pasta water religiously — the starch is the secret to emulsifying the doenjang sauce into a silky coating rather than a clumpy paste.
  • For extra depth, add a splash of soy sauce or a drizzle of sesame oil at the end. Doenjang pairs beautifully with both.
