Beef Rendang (Indonesian Coconut & Spice Stew)

Why This Recipe

Regularly voted the worlds best dish, and peak autumn: 3 hours of patient simmering where the coconut milk thickens, darkens and splits into oil so the beef fries in its own mahogany sauce. Brand-new technique for you, and it tastes even better the next day.

Ingredients

  • 1.2kg beef chuck or shin, cut into 4cm chunks
  • 800ml full-fat coconut milk (2 x 400ml tins)
  • 8 shallots, roughly chopped
  • 6 cloves garlic
  • 4cm piece fresh ginger
  • 4cm piece galangal
  • 2 tbsp sambal oelek (or 8-12 dried red chilies, soaked)
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1/2 tsp ground cumin
  • 1/4 tsp ground nutmeg
  • 2 stalks lemongrass, bruised and knotted
  • 6 kaffir lime leaves
  • 2 turmeric leaves, if available (traditional)
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 1 tbsp tamarind paste
  • 1 tbsp palm sugar
  • 1 tsp salt, plus more to taste
  • Steamed white rice, cucumber slices and extra sambal to serve

Instructions

Prep

  • Blend the shallots, garlic, ginger, galangal, sambal oelek, ground turmeric, ground coriander, cumin and nutmeg into a smooth paste, adding a splash of water if needed. This bumbu is the flavour engine of the whole dish.
  • Bruise the lemongrass stalks and tie them into knots so they stay together.
  • Cut the beef into 4cm chunks and season with the 1 tsp of salt.

Cook

  • In a wide, heavy pan or Dutch oven, combine the beef, spice paste, both tins of coconut milk, lemongrass, kaffir lime leaves, turmeric leaves, cinnamon stick, tamarind paste and palm sugar. Stir well and bring to a gentle simmer, uncovered.
  • Hour 1 — the gulai stage: simmer uncovered over medium-low heat, stirring every 15 minutes. The coconut milk bubbles and gradually thickens; this is the soupy ancestor of rendang.
  • Hour 2 — the kalio stage: keep simmering, now stirring every 10 minutes as the sauce thickens into a rich brown gravy coating the meat. Many Indonesian restaurants stop at this stage.
  • Final 30-60 min — the dry stage: the oil separates from the coconut milk and the beef begins to fry gently in it. Stir constantly and scrape the bottom so nothing burns, until the coating darkens to deep mahogany and is almost dry.

Finish

  • Taste and adjust the seasoning with more salt or a little palm sugar.
  • Remove the lemongrass knots, kaffir lime leaves and cinnamon stick.
  • Rest for at least 30 minutes before serving — rendang deepens in flavour overnight.

Serve

  • Ladle the dark, dry rendang over steamed white rice.
  • Serve Padang-style with cucumber slices and extra sambal on the side.

Pro Tips

  • The three named stages (gulai → kalio → dry rendang) are your visual cues: pale and soupy, thick brown gravy, then oil-splitting mahogany. If you want a soupier curry, stop at the kalio stage — the same pot, different dish.
  • Rendang is a preservation dish: the coconut oil that separates out is what keeps the meat for weeks. Spoon it off and use it as a dressing on crunchy salad, or to fry an egg.
