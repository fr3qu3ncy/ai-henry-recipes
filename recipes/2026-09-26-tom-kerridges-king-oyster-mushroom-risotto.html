Tom Kerridge's King Oyster Mushroom Risotto

Why This Recipe

Peak king oyster season and the most luxurious vegetable risotto you can make — a mushroom cream gives it the depth of a meat dish, with crispy sage and sautéed wild mushrooms on top. Tom Kerridge's method, all in one pan, no cream-sauce shortcut.

Ingredients

  • 300g baby button mushrooms, quartered
  • 150ml double cream
  • 250g king oyster mushrooms, cut into 5mm dice
  • 30g butter
  • 1 tbsp olive oil (plus more for frying)
  • 2 shallots, finely diced
  • 2 cloves garlic, finely chopped
  • 300g Carnaroli rice
  • 200ml white wine
  • 1.2L chicken or vegetable stock, warmed
  • 40g aged parmesan, finely grated
  • 12 sage leaves
  • 30g butter (for garnish)
  • 180g mixed wild mushrooms
  • 1 ball mozzarella, cut into 1cm cubes
  • Truffle oil to drizzle
  • Extra grated parmesan to serve

Instructions

Prep

  • Quarter the button mushrooms, dice the king oyster mushrooms into 5mm cubes, dice the shallots, chop the garlic, grate the parmesan and warm the stock in a saucepan.
  • Cut the mozzarella into 1cm cubes and lay out the sage leaves.

Mushroom Cream

  • Add the quartered button mushrooms and double cream to a pan over medium heat and bring to a simmer.
  • Cover, turn the heat down and cook for around 5 minutes, until the mushrooms are tender.
  • Blend the mushroom-cream mixture until smooth, then pass it through a sieve into a clean pan.
  • Bring the mushroom cream back to a simmer, add the diced king oyster mushrooms and stir well.
  • Simmer gently for another 5-6 minutes until the king oysters are tender. Taste, season, and set aside.

Risotto

  • Heat the 30g butter and 1 tbsp olive oil in a high-sided sauté pan over medium heat. When the butter is foaming, add the shallots and garlic and cook for just 1 minute until softened.
  • Add the Carnaroli rice and stir for 2-3 minutes until the grains are well toasted.
  • Pour in the white wine and stir until it has completely reduced to a glaze.
  • Add a ladleful of warm stock and reduce the heat to a simmer. When most of the stock has evaporated, add another ladleful.
  • Continue this way for 10-12 minutes, or until three-quarters of the stock is absorbed, then taste for bite. If the rice is tender with a slight bite, take it off the heat. If still firm, add more stock and cook until tender.
  • Take off the heat and put a lid on the pan.

Garnish

  • Heat a little olive oil in a small frying pan over medium-high heat. Add the sage leaves and fry until crispy. Lift out onto kitchen paper.
  • Return the pan to the heat and add the 30g butter. Once foaming, add the mixed wild mushrooms with some seasoning and cook until golden, 3-4 minutes. Set aside.

Serve

  • Remove the lid from the rice pan and stir in the mushroom cream and parmesan. Gently stir in half the mozzarella.
  • Sprinkle the remaining mozzarella equally into serving bowls.
  • Ladle the risotto into the bowls with a little extra parmesan, spoon over the sautéed wild mushrooms, top with crispy sage leaves and drizzle with truffle oil.

Pro Tips

  • Carnaroli is the key — it holds its shape better than Arborio while absorbing the mushroom cream, giving you that slight bite (al dente) Tom wants. Toasting the dry rice for 2-3 minutes before the wine is what stops it going gummy.
  • Keep the stock hot, not boiling — adding cold stock shocks the rice and breaks the starch emulsion, which is where the creaminess comes from. If you have no truffle oil, a few drops of white truffle honey or a proper parmesan rind stirred in at the end does the same job.
