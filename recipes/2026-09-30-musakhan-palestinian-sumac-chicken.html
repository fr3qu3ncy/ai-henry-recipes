Musakhan: Palestinian Sumac Chicken

Why This Recipe

One of the most iconic dishes in Palestinian cuisine and a genuine novelty here — a whole chicken poached in spiced broth, then broiled over a mountain of sumac and pomegranate caramelised onions and served on crispy flatbread. Perfect late-autumn sharing dinner.

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp olive oil, plus more for the onions
  • 1 medium onion, diced
  • 1 whole chicken, cut into 8 pieces (bone-in)
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 3 cardamom pods, crushed
  • Enough boiling water to cover the chicken
  • 3/4-1 cup olive oil (for the onions)
  • 5 large yellow onions (about 950g), thinly sliced
  • 1 tbsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp ground black pepper
  • 1 tbsp ground allspice
  • 2 tbsp ground sumac, plus extra for serving
  • 1 cup (175g) blanched roasted almonds
  • 6-8 large pieces of taboon, Greek, or naan flatbread

Instructions

Prep

  • Cut the whole chicken into 8 bone-in pieces.
  • Thinly slice the 5 yellow onions for the caramelised onion mixture.
  • Set out the flatbread pieces and pre-heat the oven to 350F (180C).
  • Toast the blanched almonds in a dry pan until golden, then set aside.

Cook the chicken

  • Heat 2 tbsp olive oil in a deep pot and saute the diced onion for a couple of minutes until translucent.
  • Add the chicken pieces and brown them on all sides.
  • Add the salt, black pepper, bay leaves and crushed cardamom.
  • Cover with boiling water, bring to a boil, then reduce heat and simmer until the chicken is fully cooked (about 35-40 min).
  • Remove the chicken and set aside. Strain the broth and keep it - you will need about 1 1/2 cups.

Make the sumac onions

  • In a large skillet over medium heat, pour in the olive oil and add the sliced onions.
  • Cook until lightly caramelised, about 5 minutes.
  • Add the cumin, salt, black pepper and allspice, and stir to combine.
  • Add 1 1/2 cups of the strained chicken broth to the onions.
  • Turn off the heat and let the mixture cool slightly so the onions soften and absorb the liquid.

Broil

  • Switch the oven to broil and pre-heat.
  • Dip each chicken piece in the sumac onion mixture so it is well coated.
  • Arrange the chicken on a baking sheet, sprinkle generously with extra sumac, and broil until golden brown and the edges are slightly crisp (4-6 min, watching closely).
  • Meanwhile, dip each flatbread piece in the onion mixture, spread a spoonful of the onions over the bread, sprinkle with sumac, and bake the bread at 350F (180C) until just golden and crispy.

Serve

  • Lay the flatbreads in a layered stack on a big platter and arrange the broiled chicken on top (or serve each bread topped with its own chicken piece).
  • Scatter the toasted almonds over the top with a final shower of sumac.
  • Serve with a green salad, tabbouleh, baba ganoush or a side of tahini.

Pro Tips

  • Save the chicken poaching broth - it is the secret that makes the onions glossy and flavourful, and you can drink the leftover broth as a quick, deeply spiced chicken stock.
  • Use real ground sumac (not sumac seasoning blends) and pile it on - it is the star tangy note that defines musakhan, and it turns the dish that signature deep crimson.
