Doenjang Glazed Eggplant

Why This Recipe

Eggplant is at its peak in July, and this Korean-style glazed version introduces doenjang — a deeply umami fermented soybean paste that's the backbone of Korean cooking. A beautiful, hands-off dish from Judy Joo's 'Korean Food Made Simple' that's as impressive as it is simple.

Ingredients

  • 4 medium eggplants
  • Olive oil, for brushing
  • 3 spring onions, thinly sliced
  • Sesame seeds, for serving
  • Steamed rice, for serving
  • 125g doenjang (Korean fermented soybean paste; red miso works as substitute)
  • 50g honey (or maple syrup for vegan)
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce (or tamari)
  • 2 tbsp toasted sesame oil
  • 5 cloves garlic, germ removed, finely chopped

Instructions

Prep

  • Put doenjang, honey, soy sauce, sesame oil, and finely chopped garlic in a medium bowl. Stir well to combine. Set aside (can be made a couple of days ahead and stored in the fridge).
  • Preheat the oven to 200C (400F).
  • Slice the eggplants in half lengthwise. Use a sharp knife to cut criss-cross slits in the flesh, about 1cm deep — do not cut through to the skin.
  • Brush the cut side of each eggplant half lightly with olive oil.

Bake

  • Arrange the eggplants cut-side down on a rimmed baking sheet.
  • Bake for 20-30 minutes, depending on size, until the flesh is cooked through and very tender when pierced with a knife.

Glaze

  • Flip the eggplants so the flesh side faces up.
  • Brush generously with the doenjang glaze — you won't need all of it, just coat the flesh evenly.
  • Place under the broiler for 3-4 minutes until the glaze starts to caramelize and bubble. Watch closely so it doesn't burn.

Serve

  • Sprinkle with thinly sliced spring onions and sesame seeds.
  • Serve warm over steamed white rice. Use kitchen shears to cut the eggplant into bite-size pieces at the table.

Pro Tips

  • The criss-cross scoring is key — it helps the eggplant cook evenly and creates pockets that soak up the doenjang glaze.
  • Save the leftover doenjang glaze — it makes an excellent marinade for chicken, lamb, or tofu, or stir it into a quick broth for soup.
