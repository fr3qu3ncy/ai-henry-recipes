Seared Squid with White Beans & Fennel

Why This Recipe

King squid season is peaking right now — the tubes are cheap, sweet and tender — and this 30-minute Mediterranean one-pot is a proper change of pace: high-heat searing instead of frying, with a garlicky cannellini-and-fennel base that soaks up everything.

Ingredients

  • 3 tbsp olive oil, plus extra for brushing
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 3 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 1 fennel bulb, halved lengthways and thinly sliced crossways
  • 1 tsp fennel seeds, lightly crushed
  • 100ml dry white wine
  • 250g cooked dried white beans (cannellini), drained (or 2 x 400g tins, drained)
  • 100g tinned chopped tomatoes
  • 500g squid tubes (cleaned weight — ask your fishmonger to prep it)
  • Lemon wedges, for squeezing
  • For the parsley oil: 3 tbsp very finely chopped fresh flatleaf parsley
  • 75ml extra-virgin olive oil

Instructions

Prep

  • Whisk the parsley oil ingredients together in a small bowl with a fork or balloon whisk, whisking in a little water to loosen the mixture. Taste, season, then set aside.
  • Cut open one side of each squid tube and lay it flat, skin-side up. Pat dry with kitchen paper, then pull off the thin membrane from the skin.
  • Using a sharp knife, score a lattice pattern across the surface — take care not to cut right through — then cut the squid into 3.5cm square pieces.
  • Toss the squid in a bowl with the remaining 1 tbsp oil and salt and pepper until coated.

Cook

  • In a large frying pan, heat 2 tbsp of the olive oil over medium heat and fry the onion for about 5 minutes until softened.
  • Add the garlic, sliced fennel and fennel seeds, then cook gently for another 10-15 minutes until the vegetables are soft.
  • Pour in the white wine and cook for a few minutes until about half the liquid has evaporated.
  • Stir in the drained white beans and chopped tomatoes, season with salt and pepper, then leave to simmer while you sear the squid.
  • Heat a non-stick frying pan over high heat until it's really hot, then brush with a little oil.
  • Sear the squid for 1 minute on each side until lightly browned, working in 2 batches — don't overcrowd the pan or the squid will stew instead of sear.

Serve

  • Spoon a quarter of the bean and fennel mixture onto each plate and top with the seared squid.
  • Drizzle over some of the parsley oil and squeeze over a little lemon juice to serve.

Pro Tips

  • The whole sear-or-stew trick lives in the batch size: keep the pan hot and the squid in 2 batches so each piece browns in about a minute. Overcrowd it and it turns rubbery.
  • No squid tubes at the shop? Squid rings work fine — just drop the searing time to 30-60 seconds per side so they stay tender.
