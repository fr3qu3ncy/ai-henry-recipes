Oden (Japanese Winter Fish Cake Stew)

Why This Recipe

Peak oden season in Japan — a gentle dashi-and-soy simmer of fish cakes, daikon and eggs that gets even better as it sits. Deep winter comfort food with a completely new technique for your rotation.

Ingredients

  • 4 inches daikon radish
  • 1 package konnyaku (konjac) (9 oz / 255 g)
  • 1 package oden set — assorted Japanese fish cakes and fish balls (about 27 oz / 750 g)
  • 1 package deep-fried firm tofu cutlet (atsuage) (6.5 oz / 184 g)
  • 4 hard-boiled eggs (boiled 8 minutes, cooled, peeled)
  • 1 package hanpen (fish cake) (4.6 oz / 130 g)
  • 4 pieces dried knotted kombu (or knot strips of hydrated kombu)
  • 6 cups dashi (Japanese soup stock — strong flavour)
  • 2 tbsp usukuchi (light) soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp sake
  • 2 tbsp mirin
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • Karashi hot mustard (optional, to serve)

Instructions

Prep

  • Peel 4 inches of daikon, slice into 1-inch (2.5 cm) rounds, and use a knife to round off the sharp edges (mentori technique) so they don't break. Score a cross about 1/4 inch deep on one flat side of each slice to help them absorb flavour.
  • Crisscross-score one side of the konnyaku (1/8 inch deep, 1/8 inch apart), then cut into thirds crosswise and each third diagonally into triangles.
  • Blanch the konnyaku in boiling water for 3 minutes to remove its smell, then drain.
  • Drop the whole oden set (fish cakes and fish balls) into boiling water for 15-30 seconds to draw off excess deep-frying oil, then remove and set aside. Repeat a quick 15-30 second blanch for the atsuage tofu cutlets if you're using them.

Cook

  • Cook the daikon rounds starting in COLD water, uncovered, over medium heat for about 20 minutes until just tender. Starting cold lets the centre cook evenly and removes bitterness; drain and rinse under running water until white.
  • Cut the larger fish cakes into convenient sizes: big satsuma age into quarters, chikuwa diagonally in half, fish balls in half, atsuage into bite-sized triangles, and hanpen into quartered diagonal pieces.
  • In a donabe or large shallow pot, combine the dashi, usukuchi soy sauce, sake, mirin and salt; mix well.
  • Add the daikon, konnyaku and hard-boiled eggs. Bring to a boil, then reduce to low to maintain a gentle simmer. Cook uncovered for 20 minutes.
  • If the broth level looks too high, ladle some off into a bowl to keep it from overflowing later.
  • Add the fish cakes, atsuage and knotted kombu and simmer for 10 minutes.
  • Finally add the hanpen (it dissolves if overcooked) and simmer for just 3 minutes more.

Serve

  • Serve hot straight from the pot, family style, with karashi hot mustard (2 tsp mustard + 1 tbsp hot water, steamed under a covered bowl for 5 minutes) on the side.
  • Or switch off the heat and let the oden cool in the broth — like all nimono, it deepens as it sits and reheats beautifully next day.

Pro Tips

  • Start the daikon in cold water and go slowly — it comes out pure white, sweet and bitter-free instead of cloudy and soapy-tasting.
  • Hanpen is the one ingredient you can't overcook: add it in the last 3 minutes, because it softens and can dissolve right into the broth if left in longer.
