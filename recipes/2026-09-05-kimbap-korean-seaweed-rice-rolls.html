Kimbap (Korean Seaweed Rice Rolls)

Why This Recipe

Gimbap is the Korean answer to sushi — seasoned sushi rice, danmuji, seasoned spinach, omelette and hot dog rolled in nori and sliced into neat rounds. A genuinely new technique for you that fits your Korean phase perfectly, and it's a brilliant lunchbox dish for the weekend.

Ingredients

  • 500g cooked sushi rice
  • 6 gim or nori seaweed sheets
  • 1 carrot, cut into thin matchsticks
  • 2 eggs
  • 100g danmuji (Korean pickled daikon) — or gherkins/cucumber
  • 60g seafood sticks, sliced into thin lengths
  • 2 hot dogs, sliced lengthwise into quarters
  • 100g baby spinach
  • 2 tsp vegetable oil
  • 1 1/2 tsp sesame oil plus extra for brushing
  • 1/2 tsp sesame seeds plus extra for sprinkling
  • 2 tsp sesame seeds
  • 1/2 tsp salt

Instructions

Prep

  • Put the spinach in a heatproof bowl, pour over boiling water and push the leaves under with a spoon so they all wilt. Drain, rinse under cold water and squeeze out excess liquid. Toss with 1 tsp sesame oil, a pinch of salt and 1/2 tsp sesame seeds.
  • Mix the sushi rice with 1 1/2 tsp sesame oil, 2 tsp sesame seeds and 1/2 tsp salt in a large bowl.
  • Heat half the vegetable oil in a frying pan over medium heat and fry the carrot matchsticks for 2 minutes until slightly softened. Set aside on a plate.

Cook

  • Wipe the pan clean and heat the remaining oil over high heat. Whisk the eggs lightly, pour into the pan and swirl to coat the base. Cook briefly until just set, then transfer to a board and slice into strips about 1/2 cm thick.
  • Lay out all the fillings — danmuji, seafood sticks, hot dog quarters, carrots, omelette strips and seasoned spinach — on one tray so you can build the rolls quickly.

Roll

  • Lay a seaweed sheet shiny-side down on a board. Spread a quarter of the rice over three-quarters of the sheet in an even layer, leaving the top quarter bare.
  • Cut another seaweed sheet in half and lay it across the middle of the rice — this forms the base layer for the fillings. Arrange a few pinches of each filling across its length.
  • Lift the bottom edge of the larger sheet up and over the fillings, then roll tightly, tucking everything in with your fingers as you go. Seal the end with a few grains of rice pressed across the top.
  • Lightly brush with sesame oil, sprinkle with sesame seeds, and repeat to make all 4 rolls.

Serve

  • Slice each roll into 1 cm-thick circles with a sharp knife — if the blade sticks, wipe it with kitchen paper and a drop of sesame oil for neat slices. Trim the rough edges if needed.

Pro Tips

  • The wet-rice seal is the pro move — pressing a few grains across the end of the roll glues it shut better than any technique, and it's what keeps the slices from falling apart.
  • Wet-wipe your knife between cuts (kitchen paper + a drop of sesame oil) and the slices come out clean every time. Skipping this is the #1 reason kimbap slices look ragged.
