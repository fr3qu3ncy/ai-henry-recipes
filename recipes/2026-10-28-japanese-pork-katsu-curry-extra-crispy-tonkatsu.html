Japanese Pork Katsu Curry (Extra Crispy Tonkatsu)

Why This Recipe

The ultimate Japanese comfort food: shatteringly crisp panko-crusted pork cutlet over rich curry sauce and short-grain rice. Double-textured, deeply savoury and a proper cold-weather weeknight staple — the batter-and-double-coat technique is a skill worth stealing for any cutlet.

Ingredients

  • 4 pork chops (~200g each)
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • 1/2 cup (60g) plain flour, plus more for the batter
  • 1/4 cup (60ml) cold water
  • 2 eggs
  • 2 cups (120g) panko breadcrumbs
  • Vegetable oil, for frying
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 1 carrot, cubed
  • 2 potatoes, cubed
  • 1 pack (13 cubes) Japanese curry cubes (medium heat)
  • 3 cups (720ml) water
  • 2 tbsp ground black pepper
  • Japanese short-grain rice, cooked, to serve

Instructions

Prep

  • Cut through the fat on the edges of each pork chop so it doesn't curl in the pan, then flatten the meat with the back of a knife or meat mallet to an even ~1cm thickness. Season both sides with salt and pepper.
  • In a bowl, whisk the flour, cold water and 2 eggs into a thick but smooth batter — add a splash more flour or water until it coats a spoon thickly.
  • Cut the carrot and potatoes into 1cm cubes and slice the onion.

Bread the Katsu

  • Dip each chop in the batter, letting the excess drip off, then press it firmly into the panko so every surface is fully coated. Set on a rack or plate.
  • Heat ~1.5cm of oil in a large heavy pan over medium-high until it shimmers — test with a scrap of panko: if it floats and sizzles within 5 seconds, it's ready.

Fry

  • Fry the cutlets for 3-4 minutes per side, until deep golden brown and crispy — the internal temperature should hit 72°C.
  • Transfer to a wire rack or paper-towel-lined plate and rest while you make the curry. Keep the pan oil warm if you're doing a second batch.

Curry Sauce

  • Drizzle oil in a large pot over medium-high heat. Add the sliced onion and sauté 4-5 minutes, until soft and translucent.
  • Add the carrot and potato cubes and sauté 1-2 minutes.
  • Pour in 3 cups water, bring to a simmer and cook gently for 10 minutes until the vegetables are almost tender.
  • Break the curry cubes into the pot and stir until fully dissolved and the sauce turns a deep amber.
  • Stir in 2 tbsp black pepper and simmer 10-15 minutes until the sauce thickens to a glossy, spoon-coating consistency — it will thicken further as it stands.

Serve

  • Slice the katsu into 2cm strips on a bias.
  • Spoon hot rice onto each plate, lay the cutlet alongside, and ladle the curry sauce on top. Finish with a pinch of extra black pepper or a slice of fukujinzuke (red pickled daikon) if you have it.

Pro Tips

  • The cold water in the batter is the secret to the 'extra crispy' panko — it keeps the flour from forming a tough glue layer, so the coating shatters instead of getting soggy the moment the curry touches it.
  • Make the curry a day ahead — Japanese curry genuinely tastes better next day as the spices meld. Reheat gently while you fry the cutlets for the perfect textural contrast.
