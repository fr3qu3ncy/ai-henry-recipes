Pork with Quinces (Traditional Greek Autumn Stew)

Why This Recipe

Quince is at the peak of its short November season, and this classic Greek slow-cook is the perfect way to use it — the fruit melts into a glossy, aromatic sauce that cuts through the pork. A one-pot, hands-off stew that tastes even better the next day.

Ingredients

  • 1.2-1.5 kg pork shoulder or leg, cut into large chunks
  • 3 firm, ripe quinces
  • 2 large onions, finely chopped
  • 1 glass (200 ml) dry white wine
  • 3-4 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tbsp butter (optional)
  • 1 tbsp honey (or 1 tsp sugar)
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 3-4 whole cloves
  • Salt & black pepper
  • 1-2 glasses (200-400 ml) beef or chicken broth
  • Lemon juice (for the quince water)

Instructions

Prep

  • Cut the pork into large chunks. Wash the quinces well (traditionally left unpeeled) and cut into thick chunks so they keep their shape.
  • Place the quince chunks in a bowl of cold water with a splash of lemon juice so they don't brown while you cook.

Cook

  • Heat the olive oil and butter in a wide, heavy-based pot over medium-high heat.
  • Sear the pork on all sides until deeply golden, working in batches if needed.
  • Add the chopped onions and sauté until soft and lightly caramelised, about 8-10 minutes.
  • Pour in the white wine and let the alcohol evaporate for 2-3 minutes.
  • Add the cinnamon stick, cloves, honey, salt and pepper, then pour in the broth so it reaches about two-thirds of the way up the pork.
  • Cover and simmer gently for 1 hour.

Finish

  • Drain the quinces and add them to the pot.
  • Simmer for a further 30-40 minutes until the pork is tender and the quinces are soft and glossy.
  • For a thicker sauce, uncover for the final 10 minutes.

Serve

  • Pull out the cinnamon stick, taste and adjust the seasoning.
  • Serve with mashed potatoes, rice, or orzo — it's even better the next day.

Pro Tips

  • Choose firm, heavy, aromatic quinces with no bruises — never overripe, or they'll collapse into mush. The honey balances the quince's tartness and helps the sauce caramelize.
  • Traditionally the dish finishes in the oven after the stovetop simmer for extra 'melted' richness — a 30-minute 180°C (350°F) bake works well if you want to level up.
