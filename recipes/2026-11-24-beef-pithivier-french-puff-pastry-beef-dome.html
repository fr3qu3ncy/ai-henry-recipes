Beef Pithivier (French Puff Pastry Beef Dome)

Why This Recipe

The classic French way to turn Sunday roast leftovers into something that looks like a Christmas centrepiece — a golden puff pastry dome of shredded beef, vegetables and wholegrain mustard, done in 40 minutes with zero new shopping. Peak timing for the run-up to the big days.

Ingredients

  • 300g leftover cooked beef, cut into thin slices
  • 200g leftover cooked vegetables, roughly chopped
  • 1 tbsp chopped flatleaf parsley
  • 2 tsp wholegrain mustard
  • 50ml leftover beef gravy, plus extra to serve
  • 2 x 375g ready-made all-butter puff pastry
  • 2 free-range egg yolks, beaten
  • Plain flour, for dusting
  • Sea salt and freshly ground black pepper

Instructions

Prep

  • Preheat the oven to 220C/200C fan/Gas 7. Line a large baking sheet with silicone baking paper.
  • Mix the chopped vegetables and parsley in a bowl, season with salt and pepper, then bind together with the 50ml gravy — squash it into a mass that holds its shape.
  • Roll out one sheet of pastry on a lightly floured surface to 3mm thick. Upturn a dinner plate and cut a large pastry round around it.
  • Find a bowl about 3cm smaller in diameter than the pastry disc, upturn it in the centre and use it to score a smaller circle into the pastry. Transfer the disc to the baking tray.

Assemble

  • Prick the inner scored circle several times with a fork.
  • Spread a layer of mustard over the scored area, arrange some of the sliced beef on top, then a layer of the vegetable mixture — staying inside the scored circle.
  • Spoon alternate layers of mustard, beef and vegetable mixture until everything is used up, building a dome.
  • Brush the outer pastry border with beaten egg yolk.
  • Roll out the second sheet of pastry to about 5mm thick, drape it over the filling dome and gently press the edges to seal the two pieces together.
  • Trim away excess pastry. Use the offcuts to cut a flower shape to decorate the top, scoring the petals and stem with a butter knife.
  • Brush the whole surface with the remaining beaten egg.

Bake

  • Bake for 20 minutes, until the pastry is crisp and golden-brown and the filling is piping hot all the way through.
  • Rest briefly, then serve the pithivier carved in wedges with the remaining gravy to pour over.

Pro Tips

  • A tight, cold filling is what keeps the dome sealed — if the meat and veg mixture is warm or sloppy, chill the assembled pie for 15 minutes before the second pastry goes on. Trapped steam bursts the top.
  • Swap the leftover roast veg for parsnip, carrot and sprout, and add a spoon of dried cranberries into the layers — it turns the whole thing into a Christmas dish with no extra effort.
