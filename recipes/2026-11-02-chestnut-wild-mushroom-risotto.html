Chestnut & Wild Mushroom Risotto

Why This Recipe

Peak chestnut season and dried porcini + marzolini mushrooms turn this into deep, savoury umami comfort — Russell Norman's take from his book 'Venice' is a proper winter showstopper with proper technique to learn.

Ingredients

  • 12 chestnuts
  • 50g dried porcini
  • 50g dried marzolini (mousserons)
  • 1.5 litres chicken stock
  • 100g butter
  • 2 large banana shallots, very finely chopped
  • 350g Carnaroli rice
  • a glass of dry vermouth
  • a handful of flat parsley leaves
  • 120g Parmesan, grated
  • flaky sea salt
  • freshly ground black pepper

Instructions

Prep

  • Preheat oven to 180°C/gas 4. Score each chestnut with a single shallow incision using a sharp knife, scatter on a baking tray and roast 15–20 minutes until starting to brown. Cool, peel, and break roughly by hand — set aside.
  • While chestnuts roast, soak the dried porcini and marzolini in 500ml boiling hot water for about 20 minutes. Drain and keep the soaking water — it's liquid umami.
  • Heat the chicken stock in a large pan to a very gentle bubble and keep it simmering with a ladle ready.

Cook

  • Melt half the butter in a large heavy-bottomed pan over medium heat. Add the chopped shallots with a few pinches of salt and sauté until glossy and translucent, turning frequently.
  • Add the rice and stir until every grain is coated in butter.
  • Pour in the vermouth and let it nearly evaporate. Add 1 ladle of stock and stir. Repeat, adding stock a little at a time, for 10 minutes — never let the rice dry out but don't flood it.
  • Fold in the soaked mushrooms, stirring once or twice. Then add a little of the mushroom soaking water, bit at a time, for a further 5 minutes.

Finish

  • When the rice is almost done but still has a bit of bite (test a grain between your front teeth), add a final splash of stock, the remaining butter and the chestnuts. Turn the heat up just a little.
  • Add the parsley, stir very gently, cook for a final 2 minutes, then remove from the heat.

Serve

  • Sprinkle in most of the Parmesan, turn the risotto over once and cover. Rest for 1 minute (all'onda — it should ripple, not slop).
  • Serve in warmed bowls with a twist of black pepper and the remaining Parmesan on top.

Pro Tips

  • The mushroom soaking water is the secret weapon — it adds a concentrated hit of umami fresh mushrooms can't match. If you can't get marzolini, use dried cremini or even more porcini.
  • Carnaroli is the risotto rice for a reason: it holds its shape while absorbing stock, giving that proper all'onda ripple. Test for doneness with your front teeth, not a clock — it's al dente when there's a firm centre with a creamy outside.
