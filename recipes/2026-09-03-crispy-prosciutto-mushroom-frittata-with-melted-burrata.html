Crispy Prosciutto & Mushroom Frittata with Melted Burrata

Why This Recipe

A pan-to-grill egg technique that turns a half-pack of mushrooms, prosciutto and a big handful of eggs into a rich, slice-and-eat savoury dish, finished off the heat with a pool of melted burrata. It clears the most urgent fridge items in one sitting and the whole thing is 25 minutes door to plate.

Ingredients

  • 5–6 eggs
  • 150g British white mushrooms, sliced
  • 4–5 slices prosciutto di Parma, chopped
  • 1 small onion or a handful of spring onion, finely sliced (if you have it)
  • 1 burrata
  • 40g Parmigiano Reggiano, grated
  • 2 tbsp olive oil + a knob of butter
  • A big grind of black pepper
  • A pinch of chilli flakes
  • A little salt to taste (prosciutto + parmesan are already salty)

Instructions

Prep

  • Whisk the eggs with the grated parmesan, a big grind of black pepper and a pinch of chilli flakes. Season with just a little salt.
  • Slice the mushrooms.
  • Chop the prosciutto and slice the onion or spring onion.

Cook

  • Heat the olive oil and butter in an oven-proof frying pan over medium-high.
  • Sizzle the prosciutto for 1–2 minutes until the edges crisp, then add the onion and mushrooms.
  • Hard-sear the mushrooms for 3–4 minutes without fiddling, until the cut faces are golden and their water has gone.
  • Turn the heat to low and pour in the whisked eggs. Tip the pan so the raw egg runs to the edges.
  • Let the base set for about 90 seconds, then give it a gentle half-turn so the liquid spreads out.
  • You want the bottom set and the top still just wobbly — it will carry on cooking.

Finish

  • Slide the pan under a hot grill for 2–3 minutes until the top is lightly set and flecked with colour. Do not let it brown heavily — you want the centre just barely soft.
  • Switch off the grill and slide the frittata onto a board.
  • Break the burrata on top while it is still hot and let it sit 60 seconds so the heat melts the outside into a creamy pool while the centre stays soft.

Serve

  • Finish with a drizzle of olive oil, black pepper and chilli flakes.
  • Cut into wedges and share, or eat straight off the board with a knife and fork.
  • A quick side salad or a few grapes works well alongside for something cool.

Pro Tips

  • The whole trick is not overcooking. A frittata looks 'done' too early — pull it out when the centre still jiggles and it carries onto the board. If it's fully firm in the pan it's a rubber pancake.
  • The burrata must go on off the heat. The residual warmth from the hot frittata is exactly what melts it without cooking it — that's what gives you the creamy-then-stretchy contrast rather than a uniform soft blob.
  • This clears your most urgent fridge items in one sitting: the burrata, most of the mushrooms, the prosciutto and about a third of the egg carton.
