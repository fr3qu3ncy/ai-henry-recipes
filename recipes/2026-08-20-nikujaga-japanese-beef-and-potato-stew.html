Nikujaga (Japanese Beef and Potato Stew)

Why This Recipe

Nikujaga is the ultimate Japanese weeknight comfort — beef simmered in a sweet soy-dashi broth with seasonal new potatoes, green beans and chewy shirataki. It takes barely 35 minutes, and it tastes even better the next day as the flavours meld.

Ingredients

  • 200g thinly sliced beef (shabu-shabu style or a thinly sliced block)
  • 2 potatoes (about 600g), cut into 3-4cm cubes
  • 120g onion, sliced 1cm wide
  • 60g carrot, thinly sliced 4-5mm, halved or quartered
  • 100g shirataki (konnyaku yume noodles), cut into strands about 10cm long
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 350ml dashi stock
  • 2 tbsp cooking sake
  • 1/2 tbsp mirin
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 50g green beans, blanched

Instructions

Prep

  • If each slice of beef is large, cut it into bite-size pieces.
  • Cube the potatoes into 3-4cm chunks — waxy/all-purpose potatoes hold their shape best.
  • Slice the onion 1cm wide and slice the carrot thinly (4-5mm), then halve or quarter the slices.
  • Cut the shirataki noodles into about 10cm strands.
  • Blanch the green beans in boiling salted water for about 1 minute until just bright green, refresh in cold water and drain.

Cook

  • Heat the oil in a wide saucepan over medium-high heat. Add the beef and sauté, breaking the pieces apart.
  • When the meat has turned dark brown, add the vegetables including the shirataki and cook for a further 1 minute, ensuring all pieces are coated with oil.
  • Pour in the dashi stock and bring to a boil. Skim off any scum with a ladle, then add the sake, mirin and sugar.
  • Once boiling again, reduce the heat to low, add the soy sauce and cover with a drop lid (or a piece of foil slightly smaller than the pot opening).
  • Simmer gently for about 10 minutes until the potatoes are tender — a thin skewer or knife should slide through a piece with ease.

Serve

  • Transfer the nikujaga to a serving bowl, scatter the blanched green beans over the top and serve while hot.

Pro Tips

  • Nikujaga tastes even better the next day as the beef and potatoes drink up the sweet soy broth — double the recipe and keep half for lunch or a bento.
  • No drop lid (otoshibuta)? Make one with aluminium foil: cut a square, fold the edges into a circle slightly smaller than the pot rim, and pierce a few holes — it keeps the liquid from evaporating while the stew simmers.
