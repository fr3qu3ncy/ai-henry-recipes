Nikujaga: Japanese Beef & Potato Stew

Why This Recipe

Japan's ultimate autumn comfort food — potatoes simmered low and slow in a dashi-soy-mirin sauce with thinly sliced beef until everything soaks up the flavour. One pot, almost no effort, and it tastes even better the next day.

Ingredients

  • 3 Yukon gold potatoes (about 550g), quartered
  • 1 onion, cut into wedges
  • 1 carrot, cut into 1-inch diagonal pieces
  • 250g (0.5 lb) thinly sliced beef (ribeye or chuck), cut into 7cm pieces
  • 8 snow peas, strings removed (or use green beans or green peas)
  • 200g (7 oz) shirataki noodles (ito konnyaku)
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • 500ml (2 cups) dashi (Japanese stock — use a dashi packet/powder, or water + 1 stock cube)
  • 4 tbsp mirin
  • 4 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp sake (or water or extra dashi)
  • 1 tbsp sugar

Instructions

Prep

  • Cut the onion in half, then slice each half into wedge shapes. Peel the carrot and cut into 1-inch pieces — use a diagonal 'rangiri' cut (rotate a quarter turn between cuts) for more surface area.
  • Quarter the potatoes, then use a knife to shave off the sharp edges so the corners are smooth — this 'mentori' cut stops them breaking while simmering. Soak them in cold water for a few minutes to release starch, then drain.
  • Blanch the snow peas in a pot of boiling salted water for 1 minute, then drain. Keep the water boiling.
  • Drain the shirataki noodles, roughly cut them in half, and blanch for 1 minute in the same boiling water to remove the raw odour. Drain well.
  • Cut the thinly sliced beef into roughly 7cm pieces.

Simmer

  • Warm a large pot or Dutch oven over medium heat and add the oil. Add the onion wedges and sauté until coated in oil, then add the beef and cook until no longer pink.
  • Add the potatoes and toss so they're well coated in the pan juices — this coating helps keep them from breaking.
  • Add the carrot and shirataki noodles and mix together, then pour in the dashi so the liquid almost covers everything (top up with water if needed).
  • Cover and bring to a boil, then skim off the scum and foam from the surface.
  • Stir in the sugar, sake, soy sauce and mirin.
  • Lay a round of parchment paper (an 'otoshibuta' drop-lid) over the top of the ingredients so they sit just below the surface, re-cover, and simmer on low heat for 12-14 minutes, until a skewer pierces a potato easily. Do not stir — the drop-lid keeps everything in place and distributes the flavour.

Rest & Serve

  • Turn off the heat, remove the drop-lid, and let the stew rest uncovered for 30-60 minutes — the flavours soak in as it cools and it's genuinely better after resting.
  • Serve warm with the snow peas scattered on top, over rice. Leftovers keep 3-4 days refrigerated and taste even better on day two.

Pro Tips

  • The otoshibuta (drop-lid) is the whole trick — a circle of parchment cut to fit inside your pot stops the vegetables bumping and breaking, and forces the simmer liquid to circulate around them without stirring.
  • No sake? Swap it 1:1 for water or extra dashi — you lose almost nothing. And no shirataki? Green beans or aubergine work fine; the dish is built around whatever veg is in season.
