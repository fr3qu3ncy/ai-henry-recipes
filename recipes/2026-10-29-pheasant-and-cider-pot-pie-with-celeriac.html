Pheasant and Cider Pot Pie with Celeriac

Why This Recipe

Pheasant season opens on 1 October and this is the season at its best — lean game braised in dry cider with nutty celeriac and smoked streaky bacon under a buttery shortcrust lid. A proper weekend main for six, and the filling can be made the day before so the day itself is just pastry and oven time.

Ingredients

  • 300g plain flour, plus extra for dusting
  • 150g cold unsalted butter, cubed
  • 1 large egg yolk, plus 1 medium egg beaten to finish
  • 2 tbsp rapeseed oil
  • 150g smoked streaky bacon, chopped
  • 700g skinless, boneless pheasant (breast and thigh), cut into 3cm pieces
  • 25g unsalted butter
  • 2 leeks, trimmed and sliced into 1cm rounds
  • 400g celeriac, peeled and cut into 2cm cubes
  • 2 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 3 tbsp plain flour
  • 330ml dry cider
  • 300ml chicken stock
  • 1 tbsp wholegrain mustard
  • 4 thyme sprigs
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 100ml double cream
  • Small handful of flat-leaf parsley, chopped

Instructions

Prep

  • Rub the butter into the flour with a pinch of salt until the mixture looks like fine crumbs. Stir in the egg yolk and 3 tbsp ice-cold water with a knife, then bring together with your hands, adding 1 tbsp more water only if it stays dry.
  • Flatten the pastry into a disc, wrap in cling film and chill for at least 30 minutes.
  • Heat 1 tbsp of the rapeseed oil in a large flameproof casserole over medium-high heat. Fry the bacon for 4-5 minutes until crisp, then lift out onto a plate.

Cook

  • Season the pheasant and brown it in 2 batches for 3-4 minutes each, adding the remaining oil for the second batch. Set aside with the bacon — the meat will finish cooking in the sauce.
  • Lower the heat to medium and add the butter, leeks and celeriac with a pinch of salt. Cook for 8-10 minutes, stirring often, until the leeks are soft and the celeriac is starting to colour. Add the garlic for 1 minute, then stir in the flour and cook for 1 minute more.
  • Pour in the cider a little at a time, stirring until smooth, and bubble for 2 minutes. Add the stock, mustard, thyme and bay, then return the pheasant and bacon to the pan. Partly cover and simmer gently for 25-30 minutes, until the pheasant is tender, the celeriac is soft and the sauce coats the back of a spoon.
  • Stir in the cream and parsley, then taste and season. Discard the thyme stalks and bay.

Assemble

  • Spoon the filling into a 1.5 litre pie dish (about 28 x 20cm) and leave to cool completely, about 1 hour — a cold filling keeps the pastry crisp.
  • Heat the oven to 200°C/180°C fan/gas 6. On a floured surface, roll the pastry to about 4mm thick, 5cm larger than the dish all round.
  • Cut a 2cm strip from the pastry edge, brush the rim of the dish with beaten egg and press the strip onto it. Brush the strip with egg, lay the lid on top and press to seal.
  • Trim the excess pastry, crimp the edge and cut 2 small slits in the middle for steam. Brush all over with beaten egg.

Bake & Serve

  • Sit the dish on a baking tray and bake for 35-40 minutes, until the pastry is deep golden and the filling is bubbling at the slits and steaming hot all the way through.
  • Leave to stand for 10 minutes before serving, so the thick sauce settles.

Pro Tips

  • Make the filling a day ahead and chill it in the pie dish — pheasant braises beautifully in advance and the flavours deepen overnight. The day of is just pastry and 40 minutes in the oven.
  • Pheasant is very lean, so don't skimp on the smoked streaky bacon — its fat is what keeps the meat from drying out in the lean liquor. A splash more of double cream at the end also rounds off the cider's sharpness.
