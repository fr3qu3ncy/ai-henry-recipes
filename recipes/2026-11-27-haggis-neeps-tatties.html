Haggis, Neeps & Tatties

Why This Recipe

Hogmanay is four days away and this is the classic Scottish New Year plate — if you've never cooked haggis, now's your chance. The hands-off simmering plus two mashes make it a satisfying, low-effort festive dinner that serves a crowd.

Ingredients

  • 500g haggis
  • 1kg potatoes (e.g. Maris Piper), cut into similar-sized pieces
  • 100ml milk
  • 150g unsalted butter
  • 1 swede, peeled and diced
  • 2 large carrots, diced
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste

Instructions

Prep

  • Peel and dice the swede and 2 large carrots into roughly even chunks. Cut the potatoes into similar-sized pieces.
  • Heat the haggis following the pack instructions — this usually takes about 40 minutes, so start it first so you can time the veg.

Cook Tatties

  • When the haggis has about 30 minutes left, tip the potatoes into a large pan of generously salted cold water. Bring to the boil over medium heat and cook for 10-12 minutes until very tender when pierced with a fork.
  • Drain well and leave in the colander for 10 minutes to steam-dry.
  • Return the potato pan to the heat with half the milk and two-thirds of the butter. Warm over low heat until the butter is melted and the milk is just steaming.
  • Off the heat, return the potatoes to the pan and mash everything together with a potato masher — or pass through a medium sieve for a fine, lump-free texture. Season well with salt and pepper.

Cook Neeps

  • Meanwhile, cook the swede and carrots in a pan of boiling water for 18-20 minutes until very tender when pierced with a fork.
  • Drain well and leave in the colander for 10 minutes to steam-dry.
  • Return the vegetables to the pan with the remaining milk and butter. Coarsely mash with a potato masher — you want a rustic, chunky texture here, not smooth. Season well.

Serve

  • Serve the haggis with the neeps and tatties alongside. A splash of whisky and a drizzle of malt vinegar over the haggis is the classic finishing touch.

Pro Tips

  • Start the haggis first — its long simmer is your clock. Time the potatoes to begin when it has 30 minutes left so everything lands on the plate at once.
  • Mash the tatties fine (sieve them) but the neeps coarse — the contrast in texture is what makes the plate. A few drops of malt vinegar on the haggis cut through the richness.
