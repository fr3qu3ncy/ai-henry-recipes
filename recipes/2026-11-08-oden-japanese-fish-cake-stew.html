Oden (Japanese Fish Cake Stew)

Why This Recipe

Oden is the definitive Japanese winter dish - an assortment of fish cakes, daikon and eggs gently steeped in a soy-dashi broth. A beautifully hands-off technique: mostly low heat and patience, with the broth doing the seasoning. One pot, endlessly reheatable over the next few days.

Ingredients

  • 4in daikon radish
  • 1 pack konnyaku (konjac), 255g
  • 1 pack oden set (assorted Japanese fish cakes and fish balls), approx 750g
  • 1 pack atsuage (deep-fried firm tofu cutlets), 184g
  • 4 hard-boiled eggs (8 min, cooled, peeled)
  • 1 pack hanpen (soft fish cake), 130g
  • 4 pieces dried knotted kombu (or cut from a hydrated sheet)
  • 6 cups awase dashi (kombu and katsuobushi stock, or a strong dashi packet)
  • 2 tbsp usukuchi (light) soy sauce (regular soy works, broth will be darker)
  • 1 tbsp sake
  • 2 tbsp mirin
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • Japanese karashi mustard, to serve

Instructions

Prep

  • Boil the eggs for 8 minutes, cool immediately in cold water, peel.
  • Cut the daikon into 1.25in-thick rounds, trim the edges so they hold shape, and score a cross on each round.
  • Simmer the daikon rounds in water for 20 minutes until just tender, drain.
  • Cut the konnyaku into triangle pieces and blanch in boiling water for 3 minutes, drain.
  • Blanch the deep-fried fish cakes in boiling water for 15-30 seconds to remove excess oil.
  • Cut the larger fish cakes (satsuma age) into quarters, chikuwa diagonally in half, and fish balls in half. Cut the atsuage into bite-size pieces and the hanpen into quarters.

Simmer

  • In a large pot or donabe clay pot, combine the dashi, soy sauce, sake, mirin and salt. Mix well.
  • Add the daikon, konnyaku and peeled eggs. Bring to a boil, then reduce to the gentlest simmer - oden must never boil or the broth turns cloudy.
  • Simmer uncovered for 20 minutes so the sturdy ingredients absorb the broth.
  • Add the fish cakes, fish balls, atsuage and knotted kombu. Simmer a further 10 minutes.
  • Add the hanpen (and any mochi kinchaku) and simmer just 3 more minutes - hanpen goes soft and breaks if overcooked.

Serve

  • Ladle the oden into bowls or serve family-style from the pot.
  • Mix 2 tsp karashi mustard with 3 tsp hot water, cover the bowl and flip it upside-down for 5 minutes to steam the raw edge off, then serve on the side.

Pro Tips

  • Like all Japanese nimono (simmered dishes), oden tastes even better the next day - the flavours soak deeper as it rests in the broth. Reheat gently at a simmer, never a boil.
  • Invest in good awase dashi (kombu and katsuobushi). The broth is the entire seasoning of the dish - if it tastes flat, nothing else will save it.
