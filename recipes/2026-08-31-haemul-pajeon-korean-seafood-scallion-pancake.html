Haemul Pajeon (Korean Seafood Scallion Pancake)

Why This Recipe

A restaurant-favourite Korean street food that is peak summer: crisp scallion batter with squid, prawns and mussels, fried to golden and eaten with a punchy gochugaru-soy dip. A proper new technique for you, and a great one to make with cold drinks in hand.

Ingredients

  • 1-1/2 to 2 cups assorted seafood (squid, prawns, mussels, oysters, clams), cut into bite-size pieces
  • 1 bunch scallions, sliced into 3cm lengths
  • 1 tbsp sesame oil
  • 1 red chilli (optional), sliced
  • 1 egg, lightly beaten (optional)
  • 1 cup Korean pancake mix (buchim garu) - or 1 cup plain flour + 1/2 tsp salt
  • 3/4 cup iced cold water, plus a little more if needed
  • 3-4 tbsp vegetable oil, for frying
  • Dipping sauce: 1 tbsp soy sauce, 2-3 tsp vinegar, 1 tbsp water, pinch of black pepper, pinch of gochugaru, 1/2 tsp sugar (optional), 1 tbsp chopped scallion or diced onion (optional)

Instructions

Prep

  • Prepare the seafood, scallions and chilli, draining off excess water - wet ingredients make pajeon soggy.
  • Cut scallions in half crosswise (3cm pieces); if the white part is thick, halve it lengthwise too.
  • Toss the seafood with the sesame oil.
  • Make the dipping sauce: whisk together the soy sauce, vinegar, water, black pepper, gochugaru, sugar and chopped scallion. Set aside.

Batter

  • In a large bowl, mix the pancake mix with 3/4 cup iced cold water, adding more one tablespoon at a time if needed.
  • The batter should be thinner than a Western pancake batter but thicker than a crepe batter - thin batter gives the crispiest pajeon.
  • Mix half the seafood into the batter and let it rest while you fry.

Fry

  • Heat 3-4 tbsp vegetable oil in a large non-stick pan over medium-high heat.
  • Ladle in half the batter and spread it into a thin, even round (about 7-8 inches across).
  • Arrange the scallions in a single layer on top, pressing them down into the batter, then scatter over half the remaining seafood and a few chilli slices.
  • Spoon over half the beaten egg (if using). Cook until the bottom is golden brown, 3-4 minutes - reduce the heat if it is browning too fast.
  • Flip the pancake (use a plate or two spatulas) and add another 2-3 tbsp oil around the edges. Cook 3-4 minutes more until golden and crisp.

Serve

  • Repeat with the remaining batter and ingredients for the second pancake.
  • Slice into wedges and serve hot, straight off the pan, with the dipping sauce on the side.

Pro Tips

  • Ice-cold water in the batter is the secret to crispness - it keeps the starch from gummy-ing up as the pancake fries. Thin batter = shatteringly crisp edges.
  • No buchim garu? Plain flour + a pinch of salt works fine. If you can find buchim garu in an Asian supermarket, the pancake comes out noticeably lighter and crisper.
