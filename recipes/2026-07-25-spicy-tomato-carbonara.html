Spicy Tomato Carbonara

Why This Recipe

A brilliant fusion of classic carbonara and amatriciana — tomato paste and crushed red pepper bring depth and heat to the silky egg-and-cheese sauce. Peak-summer pasta that's ready in 25 minutes.

Ingredients

  • 8 ounces uncooked bucatini
  • 3 large egg yolks
  • 3 ounces grated Parmesan cheese (about 1 cup), plus more for serving
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 4 ounces Italian pancetta, chopped (about 1/3 cup)
  • 3 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 1/2 teaspoon crushed red pepper
  • Chopped parsley and/or chives, for serving

Instructions

Prep

  • Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil over high heat.
  • Whisk egg yolks, Parmesan, and black pepper in a medium heatproof bowl. Set next to the stove.

Cook

  • Add bucatini to boiling water and cook according to package instructions for al dente, about 8 minutes. Remove the pot from heat but keep pasta in the water.
  • While pasta cooks, heat pancetta in a large skillet over medium heat. Cook, stirring occasionally, until the fat renders and pancetta browns, about 6 minutes.
  • Using a slotted spoon, transfer the pancetta to a plate. Whisk 2 tablespoons of the hot pancetta fat into the egg mixture, 1 tablespoon at a time, until smooth — this tempers the eggs so they won't scramble.
  • Add tomato paste to the remaining pancetta drippings in the pan over medium-low. Cook, stirring occasionally, until the tomato paste darkens and thickens, about 3 minutes.

Finish

  • Remove the pan from heat. Using tongs, add the hot pasta directly from the pot into the tomato paste in the pan.
  • Add the crushed red pepper, half of the crispy pancetta, 1/4 cup pasta cooking water, and the tempered egg mixture.
  • Toss vigorously with tongs until a smooth, glossy sauce forms, adding more pasta water a splash at a time if the sauce seems thick.

Serve

  • Plate immediately. Top with remaining crispy pancetta, chopped parsley and/or chives, and extra grated Parmesan.
  • Serve right away — carbonara waits for no one.

Pro Tips

  • Keep the skillet OFF the heat before adding the egg mixture — residual heat is enough to cook the eggs into a silky sauce without scrambling them.
  • Transfer pasta directly from the pot to the skillet to carry over starchy water, which is essential for emulsifying the sauce. Save extra pasta water — it's liquid gold.
  • Bucatini (thick hollow spaghetti) is ideal here — the hole catches the sauce. Spaghetti works fine as a substitute.
