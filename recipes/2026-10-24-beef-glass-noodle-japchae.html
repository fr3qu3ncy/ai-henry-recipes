Beef & Glass Noodle Japchae

Why This Recipe

A staple of Korean home cooking and the perfect next step in your Korean experiments — chewy sweet potato glass noodles, seared flank steak and rainbow vegetables in a sweet-savory soy-sesame sauce, all done in 35 minutes. Served cold the next day it doubles as lunch.

Ingredients

  • 6 tablespoons low sodium soy sauce
  • 4 tablespoons sesame oil
  • 3 tablespoons sugar
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tablespoons toasted sesame seeds, plus more for garnish
  • 7 ounces Korean potato starch noodles (dangmyeon / glass noodles)
  • 1 lb (450g) flank steak, cut into bite-sized pieces
  • 2 cups baby spinach
  • 3 tablespoons vegetable oil, divided
  • 1 yellow onion, thinly sliced
  • 2 medium carrots, cut into matchsticks
  • 2 green onions, root ends removed, cut into bite-sized pieces
  • 4-5 fresh portobello mushrooms, thinly sliced
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste

Instructions

Prep

  • Whisk together the soy sauce, sesame oil, sugar, minced garlic and toasted sesame seeds in a small bowl — this is the seasoning sauce.
  • Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil, remove from the heat, and add the glass noodles. Soak off-heat for 7-8 minutes (or as the package says), then drain and rinse briefly under cold water to stop the cooking.
  • Put the beef in a bowl with about 3 tablespoons of the seasoning sauce, toss to coat and let it marinate while you prep everything else.
  • Toss the drained noodles with 2 tablespoons of the remaining seasoning sauce so they are evenly coated, then set aside.
  • Blanch the spinach in boiling salted water for about 30 seconds, rinse under cold running water to stop the cooking, and squeeze dry.

Cook

  • Heat 1 tablespoon vegetable oil in a large wok or skillet over medium heat. Add the onion, carrots and green onions with a pinch of salt and cook 2-3 minutes until fragrant and crisp-tender. Transfer to a plate.
  • Add a little more oil to the same wok and raise the heat to medium-high. Add the marinated beef and cook 4-5 minutes until just cooked through — don't let it go grey.
  • Add the sliced mushrooms, cover, and cook 3 minutes until tender and any liquid has evaporated.
  • Add the seasoned noodles and the remaining sauce. Stir well for a minute or two so the noodles absorb the sauce, then remove from the heat and transfer to a large mixing bowl.

Serve

  • Add the cooked vegetables and blanched spinach to the bowl. Season with salt and pepper, toss thoroughly.
  • Scatter with extra toasted sesame seeds, add a drizzle of sesame oil or splash of soy if it tastes flat, and serve warm — or chill and serve cold the next day.

Pro Tips

  • The trick to this quick method: soak the noodles off the boil rather than boiling them — they stay springy and glossy instead of turning to mush.
  • Work in a big wok with room to move — if the pan is crowded the noodles steam instead of fry. And toss the spinach in off the heat so it wilts but keeps its colour.
