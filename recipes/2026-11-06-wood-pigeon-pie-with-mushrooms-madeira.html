Wood Pigeon Pie with Mushrooms & Madeira

Why This Recipe

Wood pigeon is the classic starter of the game season — first day of shooting is 1 October and it's at its prime right now. A proper countryside pie with a stock made from the carcasses, so nothing goes to waste.

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 10 wood pigeons, breasts removed and reserved
  • 1 carrot, peeled and roughly chopped
  • 3 large shallots, peeled and roughly chopped
  • 1/2 leek, washed and chopped
  • 5 juniper berries
  • 10 peppercorns
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 2 sprigs thyme
  • 2 sprigs parsley
  • 2l water
  • 340g plain flour, plus extra for dusting
  • 170g unsalted butter, frozen 30 min before use
  • 100ml chilled water
  • 1/2 tsp fine sea salt
  • 1 egg, beaten (for glazing)
  • 60g butter
  • 200g smoked bacon lardons (or pancetta)
  • 15g dried porcini, soaked in boiling water 30 min, drained and finely chopped
  • 150g button mushrooms
  • 3 shallots, finely chopped
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • 4 sprigs fresh thyme, leaves picked
  • 20 wood pigeon breast pieces (from the 10 birds), each cut in 2
  • 50ml Madeira
  • 2 tbsp flour
  • Sea salt and black pepper

Instructions

Stock

  • Preheat oven to 220°C/gas 8. Chop the pigeon carcasses into 3 pieces each and place in a roasting tray with the carrot, shallots and leek; drizzle with olive oil and toss.
  • Roast for 30–45 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the bones and vegetables are deeply golden brown.
  • Tip everything into a large saucepan. Deglaze the roasting tin with a splash of water and scrape the fond into the pan.
  • Add the juniper berries, peppercorns, bay leaf, thyme and parsley, cover with 2 litres of water, bring to the boil, then simmer gently for 3 hours.
  • Strain through a sieve and reserve — or refrigerate if making a day ahead.

Pastry

  • Sift the flour and salt into a bowl. Grate the frozen butter over it using the largest holes of the grater and mix with the flat of a knife until the butter is coated — it should stay in grated pieces, not a dough.
  • Add the chilled water and bring the pastry together with the knife, adding a little more water if it won't bind. Press into a ball, flatten to a disc, wrap in cling film and chill for at least 30 minutes.

Filling

  • In a large frying pan over high heat with half the butter, quickly sear the pigeon breast pieces on each side — just 1–2 minutes; you want colour, not cooked meat. Set aside.
  • Lower the heat to medium, add more butter and the bacon lardons; fry 5 minutes until golden.
  • Add the button and soaked porcini mushrooms and cook a few minutes. Add the shallots, garlic and thyme and cook gently for 5–8 minutes until soft.
  • Turn the heat up and add the Madeira, scraping up the fond from the pan. Stir in the flour, cook 1 minute, then stir in the stock.
  • Simmer gently until combined and slightly thickened. Season, stir in the pigeon pieces, transfer to a pie dish and cool.

Bake & Serve

  • Preheat oven to 200°C/gas 6. Place a pie funnel in the centre of the dish.
  • Roll the pastry to about the thickness of a £1 coin — 5cm larger than the dish. Brush the rim with egg and lay strips of the offcuts around the rim.
  • Drape the pastry over, cut a small slit in the top for the funnel, brush with egg, and decorate with the remaining offcuts. Press and crimp the edges to seal.
  • Glaze with the rest of the beaten egg and bake for 30–35 minutes until the pastry is deep golden. Serve immediately with seasonal leafy greens.
  • Serve fresh — avoid reheating, as it will overcook the pigeon.

Pro Tips

  • The carcass stock is the secret weapon — the 3-hour simmer and deep-roasted bones give the filling a gamey richness that plain stock can't. Make it the day before and the pie comes together fast.
  • The pigeon breasts are only seared, then cooked through inside the pie in the stock sauce — that's why they stay juicy. Don't be tempted to over-sear them in the pan.
