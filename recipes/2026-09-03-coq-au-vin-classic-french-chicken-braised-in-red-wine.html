Coq au Vin (Classic French Chicken Braised in Red Wine)

Why This Recipe

The definitive autumn dinner — chicken thighs slow-braised in red wine with Cognac, mushrooms and crispy pancetta. It's all about the technique (brown properly, deglaze with flame, thicken with beurre manié) and the payoff is a glossy, deeply flavoured braise that makes the kitchen smell incredible.

Ingredients

  • 3 tbsp olive oil, divided
  • 4 oz (115g) diced pancetta (or bacon)
  • 8 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (~1.8kg), trimmed
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 large yellow onion, roughly chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, roughly chopped
  • 60ml (1/4 cup) Cognac
  • 600ml (2 1/2 cups) red wine — Burgundy or Pinot Noir
  • 600ml (2 1/2 cups) chicken stock
  • 1 1/2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 tsp balsamic vinegar
  • 1 1/2 tsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves (or 1 tsp dried)
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 3 large carrots, cut into 1/2-inch chunks on the bias
  • 225g (8 oz) cremini mushrooms, sliced
  • 4 tbsp unsalted butter, softened
  • 4 tbsp plain flour

Instructions

Prep

  • Trim excess fat from the chicken thighs, leaving the skin on. Season all over with 2 tsp salt and 1/2 tsp black pepper.
  • Dice the pancetta, chop the onion and garlic, and cut the carrots into 1/2-inch (13mm) chunks on the bias.
  • Mash the softened butter and flour together in a small bowl to a smooth paste (beurre manié) — set aside.

Sear & Deglaze

  • Heat 1 tbsp olive oil in a 5L Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the pancetta and cook until the fat renders and it's crispy, 5-8 minutes. Remove with a slotted spoon to a paper-towel plate; leave the fat in the pot.
  • Increase to medium-high. Brown half the chicken, skin-side down, until golden and crisp — about 5 minutes. Skin side only. Transfer to a plate; repeat with the rest.
  • Reduce heat to medium-low. Add the onion to the pot and cook, stirring occasionally, until softened and just starting to brown, 3-5 minutes. Add the garlic, cook 1 minute until fragrant.
  • Add the Cognac and, if you're brave, ignite it — let it burn off for 30 seconds (or just add it and let it evaporate for 2 minutes).
  • Stir in the red wine, stock, tomato paste, balsamic, sugar, thyme and bay leaf. Scrape up any browned bits from the bottom of the pot — that's flavour.

Braise

  • Return the chicken (and its juices) and the carrots to the pot. Bring to a simmer, cover, and cook over low heat for 30 minutes, until the chicken and carrots are cooked through.
  • While it simmers, heat the remaining 2 tbsp olive oil in a large skillet over medium. Add the sliced mushrooms and 1/4 tsp salt; cook, stirring frequently, until golden brown, about 5 minutes. Set aside.

Finish & Serve

  • Transfer the chicken to a plate with a slotted spoon.
  • Increase heat to medium and stir in 3/4 of the beurre manié. Simmer until the sauce thickens, 5-7 minutes — add the rest if you want it glossier. Fish out and discard the bay leaf.
  • Peel the skin off the chicken thighs and discard it (it's served skinless, like a proper coq).
  • Return the chicken to the pot and simmer uncovered for about 10 minutes so it soaks up the sauce. Just before serving, stir in the browned mushrooms and crispy pancetta. Taste and adjust seasoning.
  • Serve in deep bowls with plenty of sauce, over mashed potato or with crusty bread for soaking up every drop.

Pro Tips

  • Use a cheap-but-drinkable red wine — the rule of thumb is that if you wouldn't drink a glass of it, don't braise with it. A light Burgundy or Pinot Noir is traditional; a heavier wine can make the sauce tannic and astringent.
  • The beurre manié (butter-flour paste) is the secret to a silky, glossy sauce that doesn't split — stir it in a little at a time rather than dumping it all in. This is what separates a proper coq au vin from a chicken stew.
