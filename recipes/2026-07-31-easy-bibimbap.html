Easy Bibimbap

Why This Recipe

A Korean rice bowl that's perfect for Martin's Korean cuisine exploration — quick, packed with flavour, and the gochujang-miso sauce brings that addictive umami kick. Great way to experiment with Korean ingredients without a complicated recipe.

Ingredients

  • 100g thin beef steak
  • 120g rice (short-grain or basmati)
  • Sunflower oil, for frying
  • 1 carrot, cut into matchsticks
  • 50g spinach
  • 2 eggs
  • Thumb-sized piece of fresh root ginger, peeled and cut into fine matchsticks
  • 2 tbsp gochujang (or 4 tsp sriracha + 2 tsp white miso paste as substitute)
  • 2 tsp cider vinegar
  • 4 tsp light soy sauce
  • 2 tsp toasted sesame seeds, plus extra to serve
  • Light soy sauce, extra, to serve

Instructions

Prep

  • Put the steak into a bowl and add 2 tsp light soy sauce. Set aside to marinate.
  • Boil the rice following packet instructions.
  • Cut the carrot into matchsticks. Peel the ginger and cut into fine matchsticks.

Cook

  • Heat 1 tsp sunflower oil in a frying pan or wok over high heat.
  • Add the steak (leave the soy marinade behind in the bowl). Fry quickly until well browned on the outside, about 1-2 minutes per side.
  • Transfer steak to a cutting board and cover with foil to rest.
  • In the same pan, stir-fry the carrot matchsticks for 2-3 minutes until starting to soften. Transfer to a plate.
  • Add the spinach to the pan and fry until just wilted, about 1 minute.
  • Add a little extra oil if the pan is dry, then fry the eggs to your liking (runny yolks work best).
  • Meanwhile, stir together the gochujang (or sriracha + miso), cider vinegar, and 4 tsp light soy sauce in a small bowl to make the sauce.

Assemble

  • Drain the cooked rice and pile into 2 bowls.
  • Slice the rested steak and arrange on top of the rice.
  • Add a clump of cooked carrots next to the steak, then the wilted spinach, and finally the ginger matchsticks.
  • Top with the fried eggs.
  • Scatter toasted sesame seeds over everything.
  • Serve the gochujang sauce on the side.

Serve

  • Dollop on a generous serving of the gochujang sauce.
  • Break into the egg yolk and stir everything together so the sauce and runny yolk mix deliciously with the vegetables and steak.
  • Drizzle with extra soy sauce if desired. Eat immediately while hot.

Pro Tips

  • The runny egg yolk is key — it emulsifies with the gochujang sauce to create a rich, creamy coating for everything. Don't overcook the eggs.
  • If you can't find gochujang, the sriracha + white miso substitute works brilliantly — the miso adds the fermented umami depth that gochujang brings.
