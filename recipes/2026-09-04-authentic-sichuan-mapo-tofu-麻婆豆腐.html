Authentic Sichuan Mapo Tofu (麻婆豆腐)

Why This Recipe

The definitive Sichuan classic - silken tofu in a fiery, numbing sauce of doubanjiang and Sichuan peppercorns. It's the 'mala' experience in a 25-minute pan, a technique-rich dish you haven't tried yet that pairs perfectly with steamed rice.

Ingredients

  • 4 oz ground pork (or chicken/turkey)
  • 2 tsp Shaoxing wine (or dry sherry)
  • 1 tsp light soy sauce
  • 1/2 tsp minced ginger
  • 2 tsp Sichuan peppercorns (up to 3 tsp for extra numbing)
  • 1 tbsp peanut oil (or vegetable oil)
  • 3 tbsp doubanjiang (chilli bean paste) - 2 tbsp for milder
  • 2 green onions, chopped
  • 1 block firm or medium-firm tofu, cut into 1/2-inch (1.5 cm) cubes
  • 1 cup chicken stock (or water)
  • 2 tsp homemade chilli oil
  • 1/4 tsp five-spice powder
  • 1 tsp sugar (or to taste)
  • 1 tsp cornstarch + 1 tbsp cold water, mixed (slurry, optional)

Instructions

Prep

  • In a small bowl, combine the ground pork, Shaoxing wine, light soy sauce and minced ginger. Mix well and set aside.
  • Cut the tofu into 1/2-inch (1.5 cm) cubes and pat them dry. Mix 1 tsp cornstarch with 1 tbsp cold water and set aside.
  • Have your chicken stock, doubanjiang, chilli oil, five-spice powder and sugar measured out - everything goes in quick.

Cook

  • Heat the peanut oil in a large nonstick skillet over medium-high heat. Add the Sichuan peppercorns and fry until they turn dark brown and crispy (about 1-2 min). Scoop them out onto paper towels - save them for the garnish.
  • Lower to medium heat, add the ground pork and doubanjiang. Break the pork up into small bits with a spatula, cooking until it's evenly coated in the red paste and fully cooked through.
  • Stir in the chopped green onion and fry for another minute until fragrant.
  • Nestle the tofu cubes evenly on top of the pork. Add the chilli oil, five-spice powder and sugar, then pour in the chicken stock around the edges.

Finish

  • Bring to a simmer, then cover and simmer over medium-low heat for 8-10 minutes, until the sauce has reduced to about half its original volume.
  • Taste the tofu with a spoonful of broth (careful - very hot). Adjust with a pinch of salt if needed; if it's too spicy, add another tsp of sugar to balance.
  • Whisk the cornstarch slurry again and swirl it into the pan. Stir gently a few times until the sauce thickens and clings to the tofu. Switch off the heat and transfer to a serving bowl.

Serve

  • Garnish with extra green onion and a small pinch of the fried Sichuan peppercorns, ground with a mortar and pestle (or coffee grinder) for that fresh numbing hit.
  • Serve piping hot over steamed jasmine rice - it soaks up the sauce beautifully.

Pro Tips

  • Frying the Sichuan peppercorns in the oil before adding the meat is the secret to authentic aroma - the oil picks up their citrusy numbing essence. Grind the fried ones fresh over the finished dish for the full 'mala' (numbing-spicy) sensation.
  • Gentle is the whole game with tofu: let it sit in the simmering sauce instead of stirring, and only give it a light fold at the end. Rough handling turns it to mush; patience keeps the cubes intact.
