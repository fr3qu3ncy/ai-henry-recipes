Brown Butter Seared Scallops with Lemon-Caper Sauce

Why This Recipe

Peak-season sea scallops meet a nutty, toasty browned-butter sauce — the same 'butter going golden for deep umami' trick that made the miso ramen sing, here on a 20-minute pan-to-table plate that feels properly restaurant-grade.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb (450g) sea scallops, fresh or frozen and thawed
  • 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
  • 4 tbsp (57g) unsalted butter, divided
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • 2 tbsp capers, drained
  • To serve: buttered angel-hair pasta or fluffy white rice, and chopped parsley

Instructions

Prep

  • Pat the scallops completely dry with paper towels — a dry surface is the key to a crisp sear, not a steam.
  • Snip off the tough white 'foot' muscle on the back of each scallop, then season both sides with the 1/2 tsp salt and 1/2 tsp pepper.

Sear

  • Heat a heavy skillet (cast iron is ideal) over medium-high until it's smoking slightly, add 1 tbsp olive oil and 1 tbsp butter.
  • Lay the scallops in a single layer with space between them — don't crowd the pan.
  • Sear undisturbed for about 2 minutes until the underside is a deep golden-brown crust.
  • Flip and cook a further 90 seconds to 2 minutes until just opaque through the middle. Transfer to a warm plate and tent with foil.

Sauce

  • In the same skillet, add the remaining 3 tbsp butter over medium and let it foam, then brown. Watch it closely — it turns a deep hazelnut brown in about 3-5 minutes with a nutty aroma; stop at that point, not past it (burnt butter turns bitter).
  • Stir in the minced garlic and cook for 30 seconds until fragrant.
  • Remove from the heat, pour in the lemon juice and capers, and give it a swirl.

Serve

  • Spoon the brown-butter lemon-caper sauce over the warm scallops.
  • Serve immediately over buttered angel-hair pasta or white rice, showered with chopped parsley.

Pro Tips

  • Get the pan screaming hot before the scallops hit it — a cold pan releases the moisture you're trying to drive out and gives you grey, steamed edges instead of a Maillard crust.
  • Don't walk away from the browning butter: the window between 'toasty and nutty' and 'burnt and bitter' is about 20 seconds. A wooden spoon you can drop in keeps you at the pass.
