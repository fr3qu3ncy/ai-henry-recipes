Roast Squash, Sage, Chestnut & Pancetta Risotto

Why This Recipe

The definitive autumn risotto — spiced roast butternut folded into creamy arborio, finished with crisp pancetta, toasted chestnuts and fried sage. Chestnut season just kicked off in the UK, so the ingredients are at their peak. Proper restaurant-level technique, and it's a showstopper for a Friday-night table.

Ingredients

  • 1 butternut squash (about 750g–1kg), seeds reserved
  • 1 level tbsp coriander seeds
  • 2 small dried chillies
  • 12 slices pancetta (or dry-cured smoky bacon)
  • 100g vacuum-packed chestnuts
  • A bunch of fresh sage, leaves picked
  • 250g arborio (or carnaroli) rice
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 100ml dry white wine
  • 1 litre hot vegetable or chicken stock, kept at a simmer
  • Olive oil, to use freely
  • 60g Parmesan, plus more to serve
  • Sea salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • Optional: 6 heaped tbsp mascarpone to serve

Instructions

Prep & Roast

  • Heat the oven to 190°C / 375°F / gas 5.
  • Halve the squash, scoop out the seeds (reserve them) and slice it lengthways into ½cm-thick slices.
  • Bash the coriander seeds and dried chillies with a pinch of salt and pepper in a pestle and mortar (a metal bowl and rolling pin works too).
  • Toss the squash slices with the spice mix, a good glug of olive oil, salt and pepper until completely coated, then line them up snugly on a roasting tray.
  • Roast for about 30 minutes, until the flesh and skin are soft to the touch.

Finish the Roast

  • Take the squash out and lay the pancetta slices over it.
  • Mix the reserved squash seeds, chestnuts and sage leaves with a little olive oil, salt and pepper, then sprinkle over the squash and pancetta.
  • Return to the oven for 5–10 minutes, until the pancetta is crisp and the sage is darkened and fragrant.

Make the Risotto

  • While the squash roasts the second time, start the risotto: heat a generous splash of olive oil in a large, heavy pan over medium heat and gently cook the onion until soft and sweet (5–6 mins, no colour).
  • Add the arborio rice and stir for 1–2 minutes to toast it slightly and coat it in oil — the grains should look translucent and glossy.
  • Pour in the white wine and stir until it's almost completely absorbed.
  • Start adding the hot stock, one ladleful at a time, stirring frequently and letting each addition absorb before adding the next. This takes about 18–20 minutes.

Combine & Serve

  • Let the roasted squash cool a little, then shake off the pancetta, chestnuts and sage. Finely chop the squash — half fine, half chunky is ideal (it'll be mushy, that's fine).
  • Fold the chopped squash into the risotto near the end of cooking, then stir through the Parmesan and a knob of butter. Season well. It should be creamy and flow slowly — loosen with a splash of stock if it's too tight.
  • Spoon into bowls and scatter over the crisp pancetta, chestnuts, fried sage and any reserved squash seeds.
  • Serve with a big dollop of mascarpone on the side and a block of Parmesan with a grater at the table so everyone can top themselves.

Pro Tips

  • The whole risotto is about the stirring and the heat: keep the stock at a rolling simmer and add it ladleful by ladleful — cold stock stops the cooking and you'll end up with starchy, claggy rice instead of that glossy, creamy 'all'onda' flow.
  • The squash-then-pancetta roast is doing two jobs at once — roasting the squash soft AND crisping the pancetta on top — so keep the timing tight and finish it while the risotto is still going. If you have a second pan, fry the sage leaves in a little oil until just crisp and use that for the finishing garnish instead of the oven.
