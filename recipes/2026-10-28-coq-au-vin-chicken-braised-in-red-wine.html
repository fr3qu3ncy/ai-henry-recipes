Coq au Vin (Chicken Braised in Red Wine)

Why This Recipe

Peak red wine season and the definitive French braise — crispy-skinned chicken thighs simmered in Burgundy with pancetta, mushrooms and butter-enriched sauce. Deeply flavourful yet completely hands-off once the pot is on the hob.

Ingredients

  • 3 tbsp olive oil, divided
  • 4 oz pancetta (or bacon), diced
  • 8 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (~4 lbs/1.8 kg), trimmed
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 large yellow onion, roughly chopped
  • 4 cloves garlic, roughly chopped
  • 1/4 cup Cognac
  • 2 1/2 cups red wine (Burgundy or Pinot Noir)
  • 2 1/2 cups chicken stock
  • 1 1/2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 tsp balsamic vinegar
  • 1 1/2 tsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves (or 1 tsp dried)
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 3 large carrots, peeled, cut into 1/2-in diagonal chunks
  • 8 oz cremini mushrooms, sliced
  • 4 tbsp unsalted butter, softened
  • 4 tbsp all-purpose flour

Instructions

Prep

  • Season the chicken thighs all over with 2 tsp salt and 1/2 tsp black pepper.
  • Mash the softened butter and flour together in a small bowl until a smooth paste. Set aside.
  • Chop the onion, chop the garlic, cut the carrots into 1/2-in diagonal chunks and slice the cremini mushrooms.

Brown

  • Heat 1 tbsp oil in a large (5-qt/4.8-L) Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the pancetta and cook until the fat has rendered and it's crispy, 5-8 minutes. Transfer with a slotted spoon to a paper-towel-lined plate, leaving the fat in the pot.
  • Increase heat to medium-high. Brown half the chicken in a single layer, skin side down, until golden and crispy — about 5 minutes (skin side only). Transfer to a plate.
  • Repeat with the remaining chicken. Pour off all but about 2 tbsp of the fat.

Braise

  • Over medium-low heat, add the onion and cook, stirring occasionally, until softened and just starting to brown, 3-5 minutes. Add the garlic and cook until fragrant, about 1 minute.
  • Add the Cognac and stir, scraping up the browned bits from the bottom of the pan, until it has evaporated.
  • Add the wine, chicken stock, tomato paste, balsamic vinegar, sugar, thyme, bay leaf and 1/2 tsp salt. Bring to a boil, then reduce to medium and simmer uncovered for 15 minutes.
  • Add the chicken (with its accumulated juices) and the carrots. Bring to a simmer, cover and cook over low heat for 30 minutes, until the chicken and carrots are cooked through.

Finish

  • While the chicken braises: heat the remaining 2 tbsp oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the mushrooms and 1/4 tsp salt and cook, stirring frequently, until golden brown, about 5 minutes. Set aside.
  • Using a slotted spoon, transfer the cooked chicken to a plate.
  • Increase heat to medium and stir in three-quarters of the butter-flour paste. Simmer until the sauce thickens, 5-7 minutes — add the rest if you like it thicker. Fish out and discard the bay leaf.
  • Pull the skin off the chicken with a fork and knife and discard (it goes soft during braising). Return the chicken to the pot with its juices and simmer uncovered for about 10 minutes.

Serve

  • Right before serving, stir in the browned mushrooms and crispy pancetta.
  • Taste and adjust seasoning. Serve in bowls with buttery mashed potatoes or egg noodles, with the sauce spooned over.

Pro Tips

  • Use a red wine you'd happily drink — it IS the sauce. A supermarket Pinot Noir or Beaujolais works perfectly; no need for anything fancy.
  • The butter-flour paste (a roux added off-heat style) is the trick to a silky, glossy sauce without straining — stir it in a bit at a time so no lumps form.
