Traditional Scotch Broth (Lamb, Barley & Root Veg)

Why This Recipe

Peak November soup — a slow-simmered Scottish classic that turns humble lamb, pearl barley and root vegetables into a thick, comforting broth that eats like a stew. The more it rests, the better it tastes.

Ingredients

  • 600g (1¼ lb) lamb neck or shoulder on the bone (or beef shin)
  • 2.5 litres cold water
  • 100g pearl barley
  • 75g dried green or yellow split peas (or 175g Scottish broth mix in place of barley + peas)
  • 1 large onion, finely diced
  • 2 leeks, sliced — white and green parts kept separate
  • 2 medium carrots, diced small
  • 250g swede (turnip), diced small
  • 2 celery sticks, diced
  • ¼ Savoy cabbage or 2 handfuls kale, finely shredded (optional)
  • 1 bay leaf and a few sprigs of thyme (optional)
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • Large handful of fresh flat-leaf or curly parsley, chopped

Instructions

Prep

  • Soak the pulses: rinse the split peas and barley, cover with plenty of cold water and leave to soak (ideally overnight). Soaking shortens cooking time and gives a smoother broth.
  • Dice the onion, carrots, swede and celery small. Slice the leeks, keeping the white and green parts separate. Shred the cabbage or kale if using.

Cook

  • Start the stock: put the lamb in a large heavy pot with the cold water, bay leaf and thyme. Bring slowly to a boil, then skim off the grey foam that rises to the top.
  • Turn down to a gentle simmer, part-cover and cook for 1 hour.
  • Drain and rinse the soaked pulses and stir them into the pot. Simmer for 30 minutes, stirring now and then so nothing catches on the bottom.
  • Add the onion, carrots, swede, celery and the white part of the leeks. Simmer for another 45 minutes to 1 hour, until the barley and peas are soft and the lamb is falling off the bone.
  • Lift the lamb out onto a board. Pull the meat from the bones, discard the bones and any fatty bits, chop the meat into small pieces and return it to the pot.

Finish

  • Add the leek greens and the cabbage or kale and simmer for 10 minutes more.
  • Season well with salt and pepper, remove the bay leaf and thyme stalks, and stir through most of the parsley.

Serve

  • Ladle into warm bowls, scatter over the remaining parsley and serve with crusty bread or oatcakes and butter.

Pro Tips

  • Make it the day before — Scotch broth is always better the next day. Once it's cold, any fat that has set on the surface lifts right off.
  • Barley keeps drinking up liquid as it sits, so the broth thickens overnight. Add a splash of water or stock when reheating to loosen it back up.
