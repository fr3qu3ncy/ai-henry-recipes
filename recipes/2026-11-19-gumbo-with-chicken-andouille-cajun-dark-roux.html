Gumbo with Chicken & Andouille (Cajun Dark Roux)

Why This Recipe

Midwinter's greatest comfort bowl — the dark-roux technique is genuinely worth learning, and a pot of smoky chicken-and-andouille gumbo is the perfect cold-weather Sunday cook for a crowd.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb (454 g) andouille sausage, cut into 1/2-inch rounds
  • 1.5 lb (680 g) boneless, skinless chicken thighs, cut into 1.5-inch pieces
  • 3/4 tsp fine sea salt, divided, plus more to taste
  • 1 tsp freshly ground black pepper, divided
  • 3/4 cup (90 g) all-purpose flour
  • 3/4 cup (180 ml) neutral high-heat oil (avocado, canola or vegetable)
  • 1 large yellow onion, diced
  • 1 large green bell pepper, diced
  • 3 celery ribs, diced
  • 5 garlic cloves, finely minced
  • 6 cups (1.4 L) low-sodium chicken stock, warmed (plus 1 cup reserve)
  • 1 tsp sweet paprika
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper (up to 1/2 tsp for more heat)
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 4 scallions, thinly sliced
  • 1/4 cup chopped flat-leaf parsley
  • Hot sauce, to taste
  • 3-4 cups cooked long-grain white rice, to serve
  • Filé powder, optional, to serve

Instructions

Prep

  • Dice the onion, bell pepper and celery; mince the garlic; slice the sausage. Measure out flour, oil, stock and seasonings.
  • Cut the chicken into roughly 1.5-inch pieces and keep it in the fridge until the broth is built. Keep the trinity beside the stove and the stock warm.

Cook

  • Heat a large pot over medium heat. Add the andouille and cook 5-7 minutes, turning, until the cut faces develop deep brown patches. Transfer to a bowl.
  • Spoon off the loose rendered fat, keeping the brown fond (wipe away anything black). Add the 3/4 cup oil.
  • Stir in the flour until smooth. Stir very frequently, scraping the bottom, until the roux reaches peanut-butter brown, then stir continuously as it passes through copper and milk chocolate to deep chocolate — about 30-45 minutes. It's done when it smells deeply toasted, never acrid; if it smells sharply scorched, start again.
  • When deep chocolate, carefully stir in the onion, pepper and celery. Cook 6-8 minutes, stirring, until the onion looks glossy and softened at the edges. Add the garlic and cook 30-60 seconds.

Build

  • Carefully add 1 cup of the warm stock — the roux will tighten into a thick paste, that's normal. Whisk until smooth and shiny, then add a second cup and smooth again.
  • Stir in the paprika, thyme, cayenne, Worcestershire sauce and bay leaves. Gradually add the remaining 4 cups of stock, whisking until the broth is smooth. Hold back the final cup for adjustments.

Simmer

  • Season the chicken with 1/4 tsp salt and 1/2 tsp black pepper. Add the chicken, the browned andouille and any juices to the pot.
  • Bring to a simmer, then lower the heat so the surface bubbles gently. Cook uncovered 60-75 minutes, stirring occasionally and scraping the bottom. Skim off obvious surface fat. The chicken should be very tender (165°F/74°C in the thickest pieces).
  • Check consistency: the broth should lightly coat the meat but still move freely from the ladle. Too thick? Add reserved stock 1/4 cup at a time. Still thin? Simmer 10-15 more minutes.

Finish

  • Remove the bay leaves. Add the remaining salt and pepper to taste — remember andouille is often already salty, so taste first. Stir in the scallions and parsley.
  • Rest for 10 minutes, skim any last excess fat.

Serve

  • Ladle over 1/3-1/2 cup of cooked white rice per bowl. Offer hot sauce and filé powder at the table.

Pro Tips

  • Judge the dark roux by colour and smell, not the clock: deep chocolate-brown, roasted and fragrant, never black or acrid. Watch the thin film left by the spoon against the pot — a deep pool always looks darker than it is.
  • Add filé powder only after the pot comes off the boil — if it boils, it turns gluey. Andouille varies wildly in salt, so make your final salt adjustment only after simmering.
