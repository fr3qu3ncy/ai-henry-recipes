Sweet Spiced Lamb Shanks with Quince

Why This Recipe

Quince is in its brief September window and this is one of the best ways to use it — the fruit braises pink and fragrant, its natural sweetness balancing the warm Moroccan-style spice blend over melt-in-the-mouth shanks. Mostly hands-off: brown, assemble, and let the oven do the work.

Ingredients

  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 4 lamb shanks
  • 1 large knob of butter
  • 2 large onions, halved then cut into wedges
  • 4 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 4 strips zest from 1 unwaxed lemon, plus the juice
  • 2 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 2 tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp ground ginger
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • a good pinch of saffron strands (optional)
  • 1 heaped tbsp tomato purée
  • 1 tbsp clear honey
  • 400ml good lamb or beef stock
  • 2 quinces, peeled, quartered and cored

Instructions

Prep

  • Heat the olive oil in a large frying pan over medium-high heat.
  • Season the lamb shanks well with salt and pepper.
  • Brown the shanks in the pan for 10 mins, turning, until dark golden all over. Set aside.

Sauce

  • In a casserole dish or large pan, melt the knob of butter over medium heat.
  • Add the onion wedges and soften for 10 mins until turning golden, then add the crushed garlic.
  • Heat the oven to 160C/140C fan/gas 3.
  • Add the lemon zest and all the spices (plus saffron if using) to the onion pan and cook for 1 min until fragrant.
  • Stir in the tomato purée, honey, stock and half the lemon juice. Simmer for 2 mins.

Braise

  • Sit the browned shanks in the pan, then tuck the quince quarters in and around the meat (it's a tight fit, but the meat shrinks as it cooks).
  • Bring to a simmer, cover with a lid, and braise in the oven for 2 hrs.
  • Remove the lid and cook for a further 30 mins, until the shanks are tender and the quince is soft.

Finish & Serve

  • Spoon away any excess fat from the top of the sauce.
  • If you like a thicker stew, remove the lamb and quince to a serving plate and boil the cooking juices down until thickened.
  • Season, add the remaining lemon juice, and return the meat and quince. Serve straight from the pan.

Pro Tips

  • Quince needs to be cooked until fully tender — it's stony and raw-tasting before that, but once soft it turns a beautiful rosy pink and releases a pear-like fragrance. Don't rush the braise.
  • The raw spice blend (cinnamon, coriander, ginger, cumin) gives a warm Moroccan note without any heat, so it's a great crowd-pleaser. Add a pinch of chilli flakes if you want more punch.
