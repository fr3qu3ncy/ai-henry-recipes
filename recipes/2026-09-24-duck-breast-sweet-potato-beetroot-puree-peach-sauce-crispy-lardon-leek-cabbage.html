Duck Breast, Sweet Potato & Beetroot Puree, Peach Sauce, Crispy Lardon Leek & Cabbage

Why This Recipe

A proper use-it-up plate: duck breast with a purple sweet potato-beetroot purée, a sticky peach reduction made in the duck's own pan fat, and a crackling lardon-leek-cabbage side. Duck and peach are a classic pairing — the peach carries the sauce while the beetroot gives the purée colour and earthiness.

Ingredients

  • 2 duck breasts, skin on
  • 1 large sweet potato, halved
  • 1 medium red beetroot, quartered
  • 3 ripe peaches, stoned and roughly chopped
  • 3–4 lardon sticks, chopped
  • 2 leeks, sliced
  • 1 head Lincoln cabbage, shredded
  • 4 tbsp butter, divided (plus 1 cold knob for the sauce)
  • 3 tbsp milk or stock (for the purée)
  • 1–2 tbsp balsamic vinegar (or a splash of white wine vinegar)
  • A few spoonfuls of stock or water (for the sauce)
  • Salt and black pepper
  • A small splash of vinegar (for the cabbage)

Instructions

Prep

  • Heat the oven to 200°C (fan).
  • Put the sweet potato (halved) and beetroot (quartered) on a baking tray, drizzle with a little oil, and roast for 35–40 minutes until tender.
  • Meanwhile score the skin of the duck breasts in a diamond pattern (don't cut into the meat). Pat them dry and season the skin with salt.

Cook the duck

  • Put the duck in a COLD pan, skin side down. Set the heat to low and cook slowly for about 10 minutes, letting the fat render out and the skin crisp. Don't rush this.
  • Turn the duck over so it's skin side up, and increase the heat to medium. Cook for about 2 minutes on the breast side.
  • Now transfer the duck into the preheated oven for 6–8 minutes for a medium-rare breast (a little longer for medium). The duck goes in the oven — the pan stays on the hob.
  • Take the duck out of the oven and leave it to REST on a warm plate, loosely covered, for 5 minutes. This lets the juices redistribute and the breast stay pink.
  • Save every drop of the rendered duck fat and the pan juices — that's the base of your sauce. Slice the rested breast against the grain when you plate.

Purée

  • Once the roasted sweet potato and beetroot are cool enough to handle, scoop the flesh into a bowl or blender.
  • Add 2 tbsp butter and a splash of milk or stock.
  • Blend (or mash) until completely smooth. Season well with salt and a pinch of sugar to balance the beetroot. You want it silky, not chunky.

Peach sauce

  • In the duck pan, over a medium heat, add the chopped peaches.
  • Deglaze with the balsamic and a few spoonfuls of stock (or water), scraping up the browned bits. Let it bubble and reduce for about 5 minutes until it coats the back of a spoon and looks glossy and sticky.
  • Take the pan off the heat and whisk in a cold knob of butter to make the sauce shine. Season with salt.

Crispy lardon leek & cabbage

  • In a hot, wide pan, fry the chopped lardon until properly crisp.
  • Add the sliced leeks and cook until soft and golden, about 5 minutes.
  • Toss in the shredded cabbage and cook until it just wilts and starts to catch on the edges — keep it a bit crunchy.
  • Season with salt and pepper, and add a small splash of vinegar. Keep it warm to serve, so it's still crackling when it hits the plate.

Serve

  • Spoon a big swirl of the sweet potato-beetroot purée onto each plate.
  • Fan the sliced duck breast on top and pour the glossy peach sauce around and over it.
  • Pile the crispy lardon-leek-cabbage to the side and serve straight away.

Pro Tips

  • Duck and peach are the star pairing here — that's why the peach goes in the sauce and the beetroot goes in the purée. Don't put the beetroot in the sauce; it turns earthy and muddy where the peach should be glossy-sweet.
  • Check the sweet potato before you start: if it's gone soft or smells off (it can be past its use-by), flip the ratio and make the purée mostly beetroot — it still works, just earthier.
